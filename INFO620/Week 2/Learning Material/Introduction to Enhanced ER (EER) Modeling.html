Week 2 ER/EER Modeling and UML Class Diagrams

Introduction to Enhanced ER (EER) Modeling

EER modeling extends the traditional ER model to capture richer semantics and more complex business rules. According to Khurana (2024), EER is essential for modern enterprise environments that require hierarchical structures and specialized data types.

Specialization and Generalization

Two complementary concepts represent hierarchical relationships:

  1. Specialization
    A top-level entity is divided into subentities based on distinguishing characteristics.
    Example: Employee → FullTimeEmployee, PartTimeEmployee.
  2. Generalization
    Multiple entities are combined into a higher-level entity.
    Example: Car, Truck → Vehicle.

Constraints in EER Models

EER diagrams also include constraints that control the hierarchical structure:

  • Disjoint versus overlapping: Determines whether an instance may belong to one or multiple subentities.
  • Total versus partial participation: Indicates whether all or only some supertype instances must belong to subtypes.

Categories (Union Types)

Some business environments require entities formed from multiple disjoint entity sets. For
example, PaymentRecipient may be a Customer or a Supplier.

Categories ensure that models accurately capture complex, real-world semantics.

Conceptual Scenario

A university may model its community members as follows:

  • Person (supertype)
  • Student, Faculty, Alumni (subtypes)

Specialization attributes (e.g., EnrollmentStatus) determine the subtype placement.
