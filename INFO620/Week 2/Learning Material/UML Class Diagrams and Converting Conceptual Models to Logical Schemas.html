Week 2 ER/EER Modeling and UML Class Diagrams

UML Class Diagrams and Converting Conceptual Models to Logical Schemas

Although ER/EER diagrams are dominant in database design, UML class diagrams are widely used in object-oriented system analysis. Understanding UML empowers database professionals to collaborate with software teams and integrate conceptual models into full system architectures.

UML Class Diagram Elements

UML class diagrams consist of the following:

  • Classes (entities)
  • Attributes (data properties)
  • Operations (methods; typically omitted in database contexts)
  • Associations (relationships)
  • Multiplicity indicators (cardinality)

In UML, multiplicities such as 0..1, 1..*, or * precisely represent relationship participation and cardinality.

Connecting ER and UML Models

A Customer-Order ER model can be translated into UML as follows:

  • Customer
    • Attributes: CustomerID, Name, Email
  • Order
    • Attributes: OrderID, DatePlaced
  • Association: Customer 1..1 — places — 0..* Order

This dual mapping enhances the alignment between the conceptual and the software engineering perspectives.

Converting ER/EER Models into Relational Schemas

Once the conceptual model is finalized, the next step is converting it into a logical relational schema. This follows transformation rules such as the following:

  • Each entity → a table
  • Simple attributes → columns
  • Composite attributes → decomposed into simpler fields
  • M:N relationships → associative tables
  • 1:1 relationships → merged or cross-referenced, depending on participation
  • EER specialization → PK inheritance rules, depending on disjointedness and participation

These transformations bridge the Week 2 and Week 3 content.
