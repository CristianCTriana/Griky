Understanding ER Modeling and Its Role in Database Design

Entity-relationship modeling is the foundation of conceptual database design. ER models provide a structured way to represent real-world concepts (entities), their descriptive properties (attributes), and the relationships that connect them. Khurana (2024) emphasizes that conceptual modeling must occur before logical or physical design because it captures the semantic meaning of data: what it represents, how it is used, and how it relates to other data.

Entities represent distinct objects or concepts within a business context. Common types of entities include the following:

  • People (Customer, Employee)
  • Things (Product, Vehicle)
  • Events (Order, Payment)
  • Locations (Warehouse, Region)

To identify entities, you must analyze a narrative and distinguish nouns that represent stable, meaningful objects.

Each entity has attributes that describe it. For example, a Customer entity may have Name, Phone, and Email attributes. Attributes can be

  • simple versus composite,
  • single-valued versus multivalued, or
  • stored versus derived.

The selection and classification of attributes shape database flexibility and normalization.

Relationships express logical connections between entities. Common types include the following:

  • One-to-one (1:1)
  • One-to-many (1:N)
  • Many-to-many (M:N)

Khurana (2024) stresses accuracy in relationship representation because relational databases require M:N relationships to be resolved with associative entities.
