Week 4 Functional Dependencies and Normalization

Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF) and Normalization Trade-Offs

Boyce-Codd normal form (BCNF) is a more rigorous form of 3NF. It addresses anomalies that remain in cases in which candidate keys overlap or relations include multiple composite keys.

Requirements for BCNF

A relation is in BCNF if

  • it is in 3NF, and
  • every determinant is a candidate key.

Example of BCNF Violation

Teaching(Professor, Course, Room)
Rule:
Course → Room
(Professor, Course) is the key, but Course is not a candidate key.

This violates BCNF.

Correct Decomposition

CourseRoom(Course, Room)
ProfessorCourse(Professor, Course)

When BCNF Is Required

BCNF is ideal for eliminating all redundancy but may require additional joins during query execution.

Normalization versus Performance

Although normalization strengthens integrity, it can increase query complexity. Designers must evaluate trade-offs between the benefits and the costs.

Benefits:

  • Cleaner, more accurate data
  • Reduced anomalies
  • Easier maintenance

Costs:

  • More tables → more joins
  • Potential performance overhead
  • Higher complexity for developers

Many enterprise systems first normalize to 3NF, then selectively denormalize for analytical or high-performance scenarios.

Real-World Example: E-Commerce Database

A highly normalized inventory system supports the following:

  • Accurate stock tracking
  • Reduction of redundant product information
  • Ease of internationalizing descriptions
  • Reliable reporting across distributed warehouses

However, reporting systems may denormalize tables into star schemas (covered in Week 7).
