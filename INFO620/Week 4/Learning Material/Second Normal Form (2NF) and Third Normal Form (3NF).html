Week 4 Functional Dependencies and Normalization

Second Normal Form (2NF) and Third Normal Form (3NF)

Once a relation meets 1NF standards, designers evaluate whether it satisfies second normal form (2NF) and third normal form (3NF) requirements.

Second Normal Form

A relation is in 2NF if

  • it is already in 1NF, and
  • all nonkey attributes are fully functionally dependent on the entire primary key.

Addressing Partial Dependencies

Example of 2NF violation:
(OrderID, ProductID) → ProductName
However, ProductName is not dependent on OrderID.

Corrected structure:

Product(ProductID, ProductName)
OrderLine(OrderID, ProductID, Quantity)

Third Normal Form

A relation is in 3NF if

  • it is in 2NF, and
  • there are no transitive dependencies.

Example of 3NF Violation

Student(StudentID → DepartmentID → DepartmentChair)

To resolve this:

Student(StudentID, DepartmentID)
Department(DepartmentID, DepartmentChair)

Benefits of 2NF and 3NF

These forms reduce the following issues:

Redundancy Update anomalies Storage inconsistency Data corruption risks

Khurana (2024) stresses that 3NF is the commonly accepted standard for most transactional databases.

Case Example

Consider the following:
Course(CourseID, InstructorID, InstructorName, Department)

InstructorName and Department depend on InstructorID, not CourseID, which causes transitive dependencies.

Correct decomposition:

Instructor(InstructorID, InstructorName, Department)
Course(CourseID, InstructorID)
