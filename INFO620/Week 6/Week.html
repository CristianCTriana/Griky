What to Expect in Week 6

Week 6 introduces a pivotal aspect of enterprise data management: database security. Databases encompass mission-critical and often sensitive information, rendering them prime targets for malicious attacks. A security breach can lead to financial loss, legal repercussions, operational disruptions, or irreparable harm to customer trust. In this module, you will analyze the landscape of database threats, including external hackers and insider misuse. In addition, you will explore authorization models, encryption methodologies, auditing strategies, and the role of security controls in preventing and detecting unauthorized access. A comprehensive understanding of database security is vital for any professional involved in the design, development, or administration of data systems.

Welcome to Week 6

Transcript: Welcome to Week 6 ▼

This week introduces learners to the foundations of data structures and algorithms, the essential tools behind efficient problem-solving in computer science. They'll explore how arrays, linked lists, stacks, and queues organize information, and why selecting the right structure dramatically impacts an algorithm's speed and memory usage.

Learners will examine Big-O notation to understand how algorithms scale, and they'll practice implementing basic operations in Python to build confidence and fluency.

By the end of the week, they'll see how these structures appear everywhere—from scheduling tasks to managing browser history—and how mastering them lays the groundwork for every advanced technique that follows.

Objectives

CO-6: Assess database security and authorization. ▼
  • LO-6.1: Describe common database security threats, including SQL injection, privilege abuse, and misconfiguration.
  • LO-6.2: Compare discretionary, mandatory, and role-based access control models in terms of strengths and limitations.
  • LO-6.3: Design a role and privilege scheme for a small enterprise database scenario.
  • LO-6.4: Evaluate the effectiveness of security controls such as encryption, auditing, and authentication in a given case study.
