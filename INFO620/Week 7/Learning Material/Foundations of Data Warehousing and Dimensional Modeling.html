Week 7 Data Warehousing, Dimensional Modeling, and OLAP

Foundations of Data Warehousing and Dimensional Modeling

Data warehouses serve as the centralized repositories in which organizations store data from multiple operational sources for reporting and analysis. Khurana (2024) emphasizes that while OLTP systems are optimized for updates, OLAP and data warehouses are optimized for read performance, aggregation, and historical analysis.

Characteristics of Data Warehouses

According to Bill Inmon and Ralph Kimball, a data warehouse must have the following traits:

Subject-oriented

Organized around major business themes (sales, finance, HR).

Integrated

Data from multiple sources is standardized.

Nonvolatile

Data is not overwritten; historical data is preserved.

Time-variant

Supports time-based analysis (daily, monthly, quarterly).

Extract, Transform, Load (ETL)

Before data reaches the warehouse, it must undergo the following processes:

  • Extraction: Data is retrieved from various source systems.
  • Transformation: Data undergoes cleaning, deduplication, and standardization.
  • Loading: The processed data is inserted into the target warehouse tables.

This ETL process supports quality, consistency, and reporting accuracy.

Dimensional Modeling Overview

Dimensional modeling focuses on simplifying data structures for business users. It organizes data into fact tables and dimension tables.

Fact Tables

Contain measurable quantitative data such as:

Sales revenue Order quantities Processing times

Dimension Tables

Provide descriptive attributes such as:

Customer demographics Product details Time periods / Geography

Benefits of Dimensional Modeling

  • Faster queries and high performance.
  • Intuitive design for analysts.
  • Supports OLAP cube creation.
  • Accommodates historical changes (Type 2 SCDs).

Dimensional models form the core structure of analytical systems and are required for OLAP cube creation, discussed next.
