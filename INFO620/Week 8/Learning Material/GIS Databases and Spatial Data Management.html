GIS Databases and Spatial Data Management

Geographic Information Systems (GIS) databases manage spatial data, which describes objects in space—coordinates, shapes, boundaries, regions, and landscapes. GIS data differ significantly from relational data due to their geometric structure and need for spatial operations (Khurana, 2024).

a. Vector Data

  • Points (e.g., cellular towers, crime locations)
  • Lines (roads, rivers)
  • Polygons (city boundaries, land parcels)

b. Raster Data

  • Pixel-based images
  • Satellite photographs
  • Heat maps
  • Elevation grids

GIS records may contain thousands of attributes associated with each spatial point or area.

Spatial queries require special-purpose indexing:

R-trees (most common) Quad trees KD-trees

These structures efficiently support queries such as:

  • “Find all locations within 10 miles of X.”
  • “List all parcels intersecting this boundary.”

GIS DBMSs (PostGIS, Oracle Spatial) extend SQL with types like:

GEOMETRY POINT POLYGON MULTILINESTRING

And functions such as:

  • ST_Distance()
  • ST_Intersects()
  • ST_Buffer()
  • ST_Within()

GIS-enabled databases are essential for city planning, logistics, environmental analysis, and emergency response.
