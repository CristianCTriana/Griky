Week 8 Advanced Database Applications

Genome Databases and Biological Information Systems

Genome databases represent some of the most complex and data-intensive systems in existence. They store DNA sequences, protein interactions, chromosomal maps, and large biological datasets used for medical research, disease prediction, and personalized healthcare.

  • Massive volume (terabytes per organism; petabytes for national-level datasets)
  • Sequence-based structure (A, T, C, G nucleotides)
  • Multi-level hierarchy (genes → chromosomes → organisms)
  • High dimensionality
  • Rapidly evolving schemas

Traditional relational systems cannot efficiently store or query these datasets due to extreme size and complexity.

a. Flat-File and Document-Based Storage

Used in early genome research; now considered insufficient.

b. Object-Oriented and NoSQL Databases

Store hierarchical and nested genome structures efficiently.
Examples:

  • MongoDB for DNA records
  • Neo4j for protein interaction graphs

c. Distributed Genomic Repositories

Large institutions use distributed clusters to store and process data:

NCBI GenBank EMBL-EBI DNA Data Bank of Japan

These systems support massive-scale, globally replicated genomic datasets.

Genome databases integrate with tools for:

Sequence alignment Mutation detection Variant analysis Protein folding prediction Machine learning models for disease classification

The intersection of databases and bioinformatics is one of the fastest-growing areas in computing.
