Week 8 Advanced Database Applications

Multimedia Databases and Complex Data Types

Multimedia databases are responsible for storing and managing nontraditional data types, such as images, videos, audio files, 3D models, and high-resolution graphics. In contrast to traditional databases, which primarily handle structured text and numerical data, multimedia systems require the storage and retrieval of large amounts of unstructured or semi-structured content.

1. Characteristics of Multimedia Data

Multimedia data is:

  • High-volume (video and audio files are large)
  • Complex (multiple components — visual, audio, text)
  • Heterogeneous (different codecs, resolutions, formats)
  • Temporal (time-dependent sequences)
  • Spatial (position and orientation data)

Traditional relational models struggle with these requirements.

2. Multimedia Storage Approaches

a. BLOB Storage (Binary Large Objects)

Stores raw multimedia content inside the database.

Pros

  • Centralized storage
  • Integrated security

Cons

  • High overhead
  • Slow retrieval
  • Large backups

b. External File Storage with DBMS Metadata

Stores metadata (file path, type, size) in the database; media files reside in external storage.
Common in:

Digital asset management Streaming platforms Medical imaging systems

c. Content-Based Retrieval

Multimedia DBMSs support searches based on features such as:

Color histograms Shape Texture Motion

These retrieval methods go beyond keyword search.

3. Use Cases

  • Streaming services (video indexing & recommendation engines)
  • Healthcare imaging (MRI, CT scans, X-rays)
  • Surveillance systems
  • Digital libraries and museum archives

Multimedia databases require specialized indexing and retrieval algorithms that are not feasible in standard RDBMS environments.
