DoD 8140: Cyber Workforce Qualification and Certification

U.S. Department of Defense Directive 8570.1, signed in August of 2004, has a direct influence on many jobs as a DoD military, civilian or defense contractor or subcontractor for the Department of Defense as it requires every full- and part-time military service member, defense contractor, civilian and foreign employee with privileged access to a DoD system, regardless of job series or occupational specialty, to obtain a commercial certification credential that has been accredited by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI). Many already have the introductory Security+ CompTIA Certification, which is required in order to have an underscore account for privileged network access to log in to the networking devices. As the level of responsibilities increases, so does the degree of certifications per Directive 8570.1.

The U.S. Department of Defense Directive 8140 replaces the outdated DoD 8570.1 and expands upon its framework to establish a more flexible, role-based approach to cyber security workforce requirements.

Initially signed in 2015, DoD 8140 provides a structured pathway for cyber security training, education, and certification requirements for all military personnel, civilians, contractors, and foreign employees with privileged access to DoD information systems. The directive ensures that individuals have the necessary qualifications to secure and defend DoD networks, regardless of their specific job series or occupational specialty.

One of the key updates under DoD 8140 is the transition from the rigid certification model of 8570 to a more comprehensive workforce framework outlined in DoD 8140.03. This update aligns with the National Initiative for Cybersecurity Education (NICE) Cyber Workforce Framework (NICE Framework, NIST Special Publication 800-181), which categorizes cybersecurity roles based on job functions rather than certification levels alone.

As illustrated in the following graphic, the new structure defines seven cyber workforce categories, including:

  • Securely Provision
  • Operate and Maintain
  • Protect and Defend
  • Investigate
  • Analyze
  • Collect and Operate
  • Oversight and Development

This shift ensures that personnel are trained for the specific roles they perform, rather than being required to obtain broad certifications that may not directly apply to their responsibilities.

Another significant change under DoD 8140 is the expanded approach to certification requirements. While industry-recognized certifications are still required, they now align more closely with job roles rather than serving as a blanket requirement. For many cybersecurity positions, CompTIA Security+ remains a baseline certification; however, additional certifications such as Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH), Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP), and GIAC certifications may be required depending on the job category and level of responsibility. This role-based certification structure helps bridge the gap between those who write security policies and those who implement them, ensuring a more skilled and capable workforce.

For those seeking a career in cybersecurity, particularly within the federal government or defense contracting, DoD 8140 has a significant impact on education and career planning. By aligning workforce development with real-world cyber operations, the directive enhances job readiness and ensures personnel stay current with evolving threats and technologies. Additionally, ongoing certification renewals help cybersecurity professionals maintain up-to-date knowledge, making them more competitive in both government and private sector roles.

DoD 8140 reflects the growing complexity of cybersecurity within the Department of Defense. By moving beyond a simple certification model and adopting a role-based framework, it provides a more adaptable and effective approach to training and workforce management.

Anyone looking to work in a DoD cyber role should familiarize themselves with this directive and ensure they pursue the appropriate certifications and training for their desired career path.
