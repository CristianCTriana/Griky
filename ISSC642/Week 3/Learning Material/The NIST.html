Week 3 Frameworks and Intrusion Detection

The NIST

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is a non-regulatory federal agency within the U.S. Department of Commerce. Founded in 1901 as the National Bureau of Standards, NIST is one of the nation's oldest physical science laboratories.

The mission of the NIST is to promote U.S. innovation and industrial competitiveness by advancing measurement science, standards, and technology.

Originally established to address the lack of uniform measurement standards that hindered U.S. industrial growth at the turn of the 20th century, NIST now oversees everything from the atomic clocks that coordinate global GPS to the standards for school bus colors and fire hydrant fittings.

NIST operates as a "neutral broker" between the government and the private sector. It does not pass laws or enforce regulations; instead, it provides the technical foundations that allow different technologies to work together safely and reliably.

NIST and Cybersecurity

In the modern era, NIST is most famous for its role in cybersecurity. Because NIST guidelines are technically rigorous and vendor-neutral, they have become the "gold standard" for both government agencies and private corporations worldwide.

  • NIST Cybersecurity Framework

    • NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF): A voluntary set of guidelines used by organizations to manage and reduce cyber risk. It is organized around six core functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, and Recover.

  • Special Publications

    • Special Publications (SP 800-series): These are in-depth documents providing specific guidance on topics like password security, cloud computing, and encryption.

  • Post-Quantum Cryptography

    • Post-Quantum Cryptography: NIST is currently leading the global effort to develop new encryption standards that can withstand future attacks from quantum computers.

Why It Matters

Without NIST, the modern world would struggle with "interoperability." Your credit card might not work in a different state's ATM, your cell phone might not connect to a tower, and businesses would have no consistent way to prove their security posture to customers. NIST provides the common language of measurement that makes global trade and technological progress possible.

The NIST SP 800-53 is based on risk management (Edwards, 2025). While the SP 800-53 addresses all aspects of cybersecurity, there is still a need for an IDS. As such, we need to review the leadership practices that help organizations with these implementations and decisions.
