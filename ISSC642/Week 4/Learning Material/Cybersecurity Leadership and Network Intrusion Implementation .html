Cybersecurity Leadership and Network Intrusion Implementation

Due to complexity and scaling, large organizations, compared to smaller organizations, struggle with implementing cybersecurity controls (Edwards, 2025). Agile principles revolve around delivery, feedback, and adaptability for fast-paced environments such as cybersecurity. At the time of this course, CIS 18 provides one of the best planned and structured controls. Therefore, we highly encourage you to review the CIS 18 controls and use them in your research development for the course. Nevertheless, and as we always advise, there is never a one-size-fits-all method for intrusion detection planning or any other cybersecurity practice.

The CIS 18 Critical Security Controls (v8.1)

The CIS Critical Security Controls (CIS 18) are a prioritized and prescriptive set of best practices developed by the Center for Internet Security to mitigate the most common and dangerous cyber attacks. Unlike broad frameworks, the CIS Controls are "actions-oriented," focusing on specific safeguards that provide high-value defense.

The current version (v8.1) organizes security into 18 top-level categories as shown in the following table. Each category contains specific "Safeguards" (formerly sub-controls).

Categories of CIS 18 Critical Security Controls
# Control Primary Objective
1 Inventory of Assets Actively manage all hardware (physical, virtual, cloud) to know what is on the network.
2 Inventory of Software Manage all software so that only authorized programs are installed and executed.
3 Data Protection Develop processes to identify, classify, securely handle, and dispose of sensitive data.
4 Secure Configuration Maintain secure configurations for all enterprise assets and software (avoiding default settings).
5 Account Management Manage credentials for user, administrator, and service accounts across the enterprise.
6 Access Control Use processes and tools to grant and revoke access based on the principle of least privilege.
7 Vulnerability Management Continuously identify, assess, and remediate vulnerabilities in systems and software.
8 Audit Log Management Collect and retain audit logs to help detect, understand, and recover from attacks.
9 Email & Web Protections Improve protections against social engineering through email and web browser filters.
10 Malware Defenses Control the installation and execution of malicious code on enterprise assets.
11 Data Recovery Establish and maintain practices sufficient to restore assets to a trusted pre-incident state.
12 Network Management Securely manage network infrastructure (firewalls, routers) and their configurations.
13 Network Monitoring Maintain comprehensive network monitoring and defense against security threats.
14 Security Awareness Train the workforce to be security-conscious and properly skilled to reduce human risk.
15 Service Provider Mgmt. Evaluate and manage the security of third-party service providers holding sensitive data.
16 Application Software Manage the security life cycle of in-house and acquired software to fix weaknesses.
17 Incident Response Build a program to prepare for, detect, and quickly respond to potential cyber attacks.
18 Penetration Testing Simulate attacks to identify and exploit weaknesses in people, processes, and technology.

Why It Matters:

The CIS 18 is widely considered the "on-ramp" to compliance. By implementing these controls, an organization automatically aligns with much larger, more complex frameworks like NIST CSF, HIPAA, and PCI DSS. It shifts the focus from "checking boxes" to "stopping attacks."
