Common Attack Patterns

Conclusions

To summarize, profiling attack patterns allows security teams to transition from reactive blocking to a proactive, behavior-based defense. By leveraging industry-standard frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK, CAPEC, and the Diamond Model, organizations can document repeatable adversary methods across various levels of abstraction—from high-level social engineering to detailed technical exploits. This behavioral approach is best illustrated by the Pyramid of Pain, which demonstrates that while blocking simple indicators like IP addresses is trivial for an attacker to bypass, detecting and disrupting their Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) is significantly more effective as it forces the adversary to reinvent their entire operational model.

Strategic planning further strengthens this posture by moving away from a "break-fix" mentality toward a philosophy of layered resilience and "Assumed Breach." This involves rigorous threat modeling to identify "crown jewels" and the implementation of a Defense-in-Depth architecture that combines preventative, detective, and corrective controls. Central to this strategy is the necessity of Continuous Security Validation. Without active testing through Red Teaming or Tabletop Exercises, even the most sophisticated controls remain unverified. By tracking maturity through metrics like MTTD and MTTR, organizations can ensure their security investments are effectively mitigating business risk and addressing vulnerabilities, including the often-overlooked patterns of insider threats.
