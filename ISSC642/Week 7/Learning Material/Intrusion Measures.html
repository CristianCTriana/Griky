Intrusion Measures

Companies such as hospitals are required to perform proper testing for intrusions due to HIPAA and PHI confidentiality (Edwards, 2025). Furthermore, financial sectors must follow GLBA and SOX for intrusion measures and for financial transparency (2025). However, if these intrusion measure protocols are not transparent, we need to have assurance that we take the proper measures set by other organizations. Every organization should follow an ISO and develop its own Security Management System (SMS) (Vacca, 2024).

In the United States, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) serves as the foundational legal framework for protecting Protected Health Information (PHI), which encompasses any individually identifiable health data—ranging from medical histories and test results to social security numbers and biometric identifiers. HIPAA mandates that "Covered Entities," such as healthcare providers and insurance companies, as well as their business associates, implement rigorous administrative, physical, and technical safeguards to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of this sensitive data.

By requiring periodic risk assessments and strict access controls, the law aims to balance the necessary flow of health information for high-quality care with the fundamental right of patients to have their personal medical privacy protected against unauthorized disclosure or misuse.
