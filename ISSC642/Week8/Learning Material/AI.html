Week 8 Future Intrusions and Threats

Artificial Intelligence

Although AI can be beneficial for machine learning and fail-safes, it is evident that it can also introduce various vulnerabilities. As criminals become more familiar with the associated security threats of AI, we will see more data poisoning and phishing emails that will bypass our intrusion detection methods (Edwards, 2025). We bring this to your attention not to scare you but rather to inform you. Please never forget that our intrusion detection methods are never finished, as vulnerabilities and exploits will always increase in number.

AI in Intrusion Detection: The Double-Edged Sword

Artificial Intelligence has transformed Intrusion Detection Systems (IDS) from static, rule-based tools into dynamic, evolving defenses. However, the same capabilities that empower defenders are also being weaponized by adversaries, creating a high-stakes "AI arms race."

AI excels at processing massive datasets and identifying patterns that are invisible to human analysts.

  • Behavioral Anomaly Detection: Unlike traditional "Signature-based" IDS, AI can establish a baseline of "normal" network behavior. It can detect a breach based on subtle deviations—such as a user accessing files at an unusual time—even if no known malware is used.
  • Predictive Analysis: AI can correlate minor events across different parts of a network to predict a large-scale attack before it fully manifests.
  • Automated Response & Triage: AI reduces "Alert Fatigue" by automatically filtering out false positives and prioritizing high-risk threats, allowing human analysts to focus on complex investigations.
  • Deciphering Encrypted Traffic: Advanced ML models can identify malicious patterns within encrypted traffic (Encrypted Traffic Analytics) without needing to decrypt the data, preserving privacy while maintaining security.

As defenders adopt AI, threat actors are using "Adversarial AI" to bypass even the most advanced detection systems.

  • Evasion Attacks: Attackers use AI to "test" their malware against detection models. By making minute, mathematically calculated changes to a file’s code, they can create "adversarial examples" that are functionally malicious but appear benign to an AI-based IDS.
  • Automated Phishing & Social Engineering: AI can generate highly convincing, personalized phishing content at scale, increasing the likelihood of an initial intrusion that grants an attacker "legitimate" credentials.
  • Polymorphic Malware: Attackers use AI to create malware that constantly changes its own code (signature) and behavior (pattern) to stay one step ahead of detection algorithms.
  • Model Poisoning: If an attacker gains access to the data used to "train" an IDS, they can subtly "poison" the dataset, so the AI learns to ignore specific malicious activities as if they were normal.

The Current Reality: Human-AI Collaboration

The most effective Intrusion Detection strategy today is not "AI-only," but rather Augmented Intelligence. While AI provides the speed and scale to process trillions of events, human intuition remains vital for understanding context, business impact, and the nuanced "why" behind an attack. The following table provides a comparison of AI-enhanced detection and AI-driven attacks.

Strategic Comparison
Feature AI-Enhanced Detection AI-Driven Attacks
Primary Goal Minimize False Positives / Speed up Response Maximize Stealth / Automate Exploitation
Core Technique Pattern Matching & Anomaly Detection Generative Mimicry & Model Probing
Weakness Vulnerable to "Poisoning" and Evasion High Compute Cost and Technical Complexity
