Scholar Practitioner Paradigm

Instead of perpetuating the differences between the two, McNatt et al. (2010) propose a new category: the “Pracademic,” which embodies the Scholar Practitioner paradigm. In this view of the world:

  • Right action based on sound theory is the focus
  • Knowledge leads to understanding
  • Evidence is used to guide action and action to gain evidence
  • Solving problems with action based on sound theory
  • Advances evidence-based knowledge and impacts the bottom line

The Ideal Scholar Practitioner

McClintock (2004) describes the ideal Scholar Practioner. They demonstrate the following capabilities:

  • Able to frame situations from multiple perspectives
  • Pose alternative hypotheses
  • Identify evidence to support alternative explanations
  • Emotionally intelligent
  • Tolerates ambiguity
  • Compassionate
  • Committed to improvement
