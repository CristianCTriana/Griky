What to Expect in Week 1

The Information Technology Creative Project Capstone course gives students the opportunity to address real-world issues relevant to their profession, plan and execute a project, and contribute to their discipline while developing mastery of selected skills. Creative projects may originate from a wide variety of options, such as standard operating procedures, training program manuals, organizational change proposals, software and hardware design or development, or other information technology solutions.

This module introduces the course, provides an overview of the next 16 weeks, and explains the rationale behind it.

Objectives

CO-1: Apply research methodologies to investigate an information technology problem. ▼
  • LO-1.1: Examine trends, technologies, and contextual factors relevant to the information technology problem.
CO-2: Analyze selected research methods and tools. ▼
  • LO-2.3: Critique scholarly research and its application to the creative project.
CO-3: Defend conclusions using graduate-level critical thinking and evidence-based reasoning. ▼
  • LO-3.3: Evaluate stakeholder concerns relative to the proposed solution.

| Relevance:

This week’s lesson matters because it helps you clearly understand what this course is—and what it is not. Rather than focusing on building and deploying a finished IT product, the lesson reframes success around developing a viable, well justified idea supported by research, critical thinking, and practical reasoning. You are introduced to the purpose of the IT Creative Project Capstone as an opportunity to address real world IT problems, integrate prior coursework, and produce professional artifacts—a project proposal, business case, and pitch deck—that mirror how ideas are advanced in actual organizations. This perspective is critical early in the course because it sets appropriate expectations and helps you shift from a purely academic mindset toward one focused on applied, outcome driven work.

The lesson also matters because it introduces the scholar practitioner (“pracademic”) mindset that underpins the entire course. By contrasting academic and practitioner paradigms, you will learn why neither theory alone nor action alone is sufficient for solving complex IT problems. Instead, effective professionals ground action in sound theory, use evidence to guide decisions, and consider stakeholder concerns while driving organizational impact. This week establishes why communication, stakeholder alignment, and the ability to “sell” ideas are just as important as technical expertise, reinforcing that project success depends on strategy, evidence based reasoning, and persuasive leadership—not simply having the best idea.
