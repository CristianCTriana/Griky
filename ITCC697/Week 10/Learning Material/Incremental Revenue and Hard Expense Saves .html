Cost/Benefit/ROI

Incremental Revenue and Hard Expense Saves

Incremental Revenue

Definition: Additional non-funds or net interest income achieved as a result of project completion, less any direct costs to generate incremental business.

Measurement Principles:

  • If increased business is achieved because of an improved process, the revenue impact allocated will be conservative and based on the amount that the business manager certifies the project contributed to increased hit rates, cross-sell, etc.
  • ROA spread must be based on the actual or historical rate for like assets.

Hard Expense Saves

Definition: Actual expense reduction or loss reduction permanently achieved as a result of project completion.

Examples (partial list):

  • Net salaries, fringes, incentive compensation, occupancy, telecommunications, office supplies, outside services, equipment costs (operating expense or depreciation) and other operating expense categories affected by a project.
  • Credit losses, errors, or fraud losses are measurably reduced and traceable to cost centers and financial statement line items.

Key Characteristics:

  • Hard expense saves are always traceable and measurable to specific cost centers and financial statement line items.
  • Personnel cost saves are recognized beginning on the date that the actual FTE is eliminated from the business.
  • If FTE saves are redeployed to other incremental positions within the business or to another business (e.g., backoffice positions redeployed to other positions), a soft save results due to cost avoidance. These are important contributions of the team and should be acknowledged and always sought. However, since they do not change TSR, they are not counted as a hard save.

⚠️ A Note on Cost Avoidance

Avoidance of costs that may occur in the future do not qualify as Hard Saves unless:

  1. The actual costs can be demonstrated to have occurred within a reasonable recent period and now no longer will.
  2. Their future incidence is virtually certain.

These reduced costs must be traceable to the bottom line and to the team’s efforts. As they are only traceable in the future, tracking these results to determine whether they actually prevent capital from being expended is typically the responsibility of Finance.
