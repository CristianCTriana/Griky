Risks and Issues

Risk

A risk is anything that can have a negative impact on the plan.  

For the business plan, you should focus on two types of risk: 

Individual risk  

Impacts one portion of the business case such as key person risk, vendor selection, or time to market 

Overall project risk 

Impacts the entire initiative and typically relate to budget 

Risks in general 

  • Can occur any time 
    • Need for proactivity in thoughtfully identifying risks 
    • Need for monitoring and review throughout the project life cycle 
  • May result from  
    • Things that happen 
    • Things that should happen but don’t 
  • Risk Valence 
    • Positive Risk 
      • An opportunity  
      • Uncertain events that, if they occur, provide a benefit 
    • Negative Risk 
      • A threat 
      • Potential losses that, if they occur, cause harm 

Risk Categories 

Consider the potential negative impacts on your proposal. What are they? 

Usually, you categorize risks by type. The PESTLE model is a helpful mnemonic to remember the categories: 

Types of risk (PESTLE) 

  • Political 
  • Economic 
  • Social 
  • Technological 
  • Legal 
  • Environmental 

Risks are an unpleasant fact. They exist. They can and do affect projects. There are only certain things to do with risks:

Accept

Acknowledge the risk and monitor carefully, usually low impact.

Avoid

Remove the risk by changing the plan.

Transfer

Move the risk to a third party, e.g., insurance.

Mitigate

Take action to blunt or lower the probability of a risk.

Embrace

Understand the risk and attack it head-on.

Risk Identification

How can you as an individual identify risks? You may know some, but you need to work with others to elicit risks you might not know. Therefore, you should work in conjunction with others to identify them. 

Methods 

  • Expert judgement 
  • Brainstorming 
  • Checklists 
  • Interviews 

Part of identifying risks is determining who is responsible to address the risk. Those owners need to understand what the risk is, and that they are responsible for addressing them. 

  • Owner 
    • Who is responsible to address 
  • Probability and impact 
    • Likelihood of an event 
    • Impact on project 
  • Detection 
    • How to know if it occurs 
  • Mitigants 
    • What can be done to address the risk
