What to Expect in Week 11

A business case includes a section that details risks and issues related to the proposed project. It identifies, analyzes, and mitigates any potential threats to the project.

For the purposes of the business case, you should focus on the project's key risks and issues. These risks and issues should relate to project risk but may also involve business risk if the project affects the broader business.

This module covers some of the key information related to risk and business cases.

Your organization may have a broader risk management regime. You should tailor your treatment of risks and issues to that regime.

Issues are events that actually occur and are managed to closure. Failure to address issues may contribute to overall project failure. Thus, issues and risks are closely related.

| Week 11 Relevance: Risks and Issues

This week’s lesson connects the concepts of risk and issues as two sides of the same coin in project management responsibility. Risks represent uncertain events that may affect a project, while issues are events that have already occurred and require immediate action. Understanding this relationship helps you see that effective project management is both proactive and reactive: identifying risks early, assigning ownership, and monitoring probability and impact can prevent risks from becoming issues. Tools such as PESTLE risk categories, risk registers, and risk reports provide structured ways to capture, analyze, and communicate potential threats across the project life cycle, ensuring that decision makers are informed and prepared rather than surprised.

These ideas matter because unmanaged risks and unresolved issues are common causes of project failure. By documenting risks, ranking them by probability and impact, and defining response strategies—such as mitigation, transfer, or acceptance—project teams improve planning quality and increase the likelihood of success. Similarly, maintaining an issue log ensures accountability and timely resolution when problems arise. Together, these practices support informed decision making, protect organizational resources, and strengthen the business case by demonstrating that the proposed project has been thoughtfully evaluated beyond cost and benefits alone. In short, managing risks and issues is not just a technical exercise, but a critical component of responsible leadership and sustainable project outcomes.
