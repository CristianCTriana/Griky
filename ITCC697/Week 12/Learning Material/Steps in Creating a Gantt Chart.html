High-level Project Planning

Steps in Creating a Gantt Chart

Creating a Gantt chart for a business case focuses on estimating sequence and timing at a high level. The steps below outline a simple, practical approach to visualizing project scope before detailed planning begins.

An effective way to begin creating a Gantt chart is to begin with right-to-left planning. This means you begin with your target delivery at some point in the future. This date is placed to the far right. Moving back toward the left, you move to the expected kick-off of the project, and then further back to today. Today is the day or date when you are delivering the business case.

Delivery of your final product requires several steps to ensure on-time delivery.

You know most of these steps:

  • Kick-off
  • Planning
  • Building
  • Testing
  • Launching

However, you may have additional steps. For example, you may have to procure supplies or equipment to move the project forward. When does that have to occur?

Once the business case is approved, certain things will be decided since you laid them out in the document:

  • Vision and deliverables
  • Team members
  • Initial project plan
  • Budget estimates

After approval and before kick-off, you’ll need to ensure these are all formalized and in place:

  • Final deliverables
  • Team member commitment
  • Detailed project planning
  • Budget allocations

For the business case, you are estimating the time that some of these tasks will take. You want them to be reasonably accurate—not too aggressive, not too protracted.

This step is straightforward. It means taking the information on discrete tasks and time estimates and plotting it into a Gantt chart.
