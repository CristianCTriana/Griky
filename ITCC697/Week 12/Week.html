What to Expect in Week 12

Project Planning is a discrete function within most organizations. For the proposal, only rough estimates and tentative timelines are stated. For the business case, a plan with richer detail is included. However, it is still only high-level and is tentative until the project actually kicks off and begins its execution. Then, full project planning would take over.

Project planning is the arrangement of a sequence of events across time to achieve an outcome. Many resources exist that discuss project planning in detail. This is not detailed project planning. In fact, business cases sometimes get bogged down in too much detail. For the business case, you are using time estimates. Before the project is green-lit, you are providing a sense of scale and scope to perform your analysis and gain support for the project.

| Week 12 Relevance: High-level Project Planning

High-level project planning serves as the bridge between an initial idea and successful execution by translating vision into a realistic, time-bound roadmap. In a business case, planning is intentionally tentative and high-level, relying on time estimates rather than detailed schedules so decision makers can understand the scope, scale, and feasibility of the proposed work. Tools such as Gantt charts visually connect tasks, timelines, and overlapping activities, helping stakeholders see how work unfolds over time and where efforts occur concurrently. This approach avoids the common pitfall of overplanning too early while still providing enough structure to evaluate risk, resource needs, and overall viability before a project is approved and formally launched.

These concepts matter because effective high-level planning builds credibility and alignment among stakeholders. Estimating time using historical data, experience, and subject-matter expertise reinforces sound judgment and graduate-level critical thinking, while reviewing and validating plans with sponsors and stakeholders ensures that concerns are addressed early. By socializing the timeline and assumptions, project leaders gain buy in, refine their analysis, and reduce the likelihood of surprises later in execution. Ultimately, high-level project planning is not about precision—it is about informed decision-making, shared understanding, and laying a strong foundation for detailed planning once a project is approved.
