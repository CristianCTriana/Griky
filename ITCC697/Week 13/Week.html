What to Expect in Week 13

A recommendation is the suggested path forward chosen from a set of alternatives. A recommendation is simply the preferred way forward from a choice of alternatives. In Week 9, you learned about the generation of alternatives. In this module, we look at determining the best way forward.

For any problem, there are often multiple solutions. Your detailed analysis of the problem space should lead to a set of viable alternatives. In the business case, you are presenting your recommended solution from among those alternatives to the challenge defined in the problem statement.

The recommendation is the argument for the best solution based on the factors you have uncovered during your analysis.

| Week 13 Relevance: Recommendations

In Week 13, the focus shifts from analyzing options to recommending a course of action, which is the central purpose of a business case. Earlier in the course, you learned how to define a problem, research it, and generate alternatives. This week builds directly on that work by showing how those analyses come together in a clear recommendation—the preferred solution chosen from viable alternatives. A strong recommendation restates the original goal, identifies the best action, justifies that choice with evidence (e.g., cost–benefit considerations), acknowledges a key risk, and outlines next steps. In other words, the recommendation is not a new idea; it is the logical conclusion of how the problem was framed, researched, and analyzed throughout the business case.

These ideas matter because decision makers do not act on analysis alone—they act on well-reasoned recommendations that balance technical feasibility, stakeholder needs, risk, and value. A recommendation must align the proposed solution with the original problem and address broader measures of success, including adoption, realized benefits, and return on investment. The lesson emphasizes that project success goes beyond meeting time, cost, and quality targets; it also depends on whether the solution delivers meaningful outcomes and is supported by stakeholders. By learning how to craft a concise, evidence-based recommendation and socialize it effectively, you develop a critical skill used in real organizations: persuading others to move forward with confidence based on sound analysis and clear reasoning.
