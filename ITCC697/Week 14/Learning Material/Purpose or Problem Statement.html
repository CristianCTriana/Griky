Week 14
Executive Summary

Purpose or Problem Statement

The executive summary states the key points of the business case in a concise fashion. Your goal with it is to capture the attention of the executives or other leaders who may not have the time to read the business case in detail.

A strong opening is a great way to introduce the business case. There is no formula for an opening statement. It should be eye-catching without being provocative. The opening may be:

Direct

  • “We have a problem with X.”
  • Clearly states the problem.

Cost-based

  • “We are spending X dollars for each Y we produce but we can do better.”
  • Uses actual monetary values.

Opportunistic

  • “We can reduce costs by X and increase revenue by Y by doing Z.”
  • Uses prospective monetary values.

Narrative

  • “Anna Analyst [a real person] spends nearly X% of their time doing Y when they’d rather be doing Z.”
  • Personalizes the problem, makes it real.

Contextual

  • “Our market sector is facing headwinds due to X, but we can face that.”
  • Generalizes the problem and specifies organizational uniqueness.

The opening sentence captures attention and introduces the problem statement to follow. In the proposal, you garnered attention with an initial problem statement. This was about the current situation, the “as is.” Given your detailed analysis for the business case, you are now in a position to refine or restate the initial problem statement, tailoring it for the business case.

The problem statement is now a clearer statement of the “as is” situation. It is more focused on the specifics of the problem. However, you still refrain from attributing causes or implied solutions. You are merely clearly stating the problem.
