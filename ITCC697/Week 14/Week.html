What to Expect in Week 14

The executive summary appears at the beginning of the business case. It provides a high-level summary of the business case. Write the executive summary last. You will have written the rest of the business case and know everything that you need to include in the summary.

Because an executive summary appears at the beginning of the business case, it sets the stage for the business case to follow. Senior leaders, sadly, do not often have the time to read the entire business case. Often, they will only read the executive summary. Therefore, it must contain the key facts and findings of the whole business case, albeit in a condensed form.

The executive summary is a distillation of the main points. Concision is the goal.

This module walks you through the details of the executive summary.

| Week 14 Relevance: Executive Summary

This week’s focus brings together the core elements of the IT research and business case process by emphasizing how disciplined research, critical analysis, and clear communication work together to support sound decision-making. You will apply research methodologies and theoretical frameworks to investigate a defined IT problem, moving beyond surface-level description to evidence-based understanding. By critiquing scholarly sources and evaluating stakeholder concerns, you will strengthen your ability to distinguish credible findings from assumptions, ensuring that conclusions are grounded in data, theory, and graduate-level critical thinking rather than opinion. These activities reinforce the importance of understanding the “as is” situation clearly before proposing solutions, a skill essential in both academic and professional IT environments.

Equally important, this week’s lesson highlights why synthesis and communication matter as much as analysis. The executive summary serves as a concise bridge between complex research and executive decision-making, requiring you to translate detailed findings into a clear narrative that explains the problem, key insights, recommended action, and anticipated impact. By integrating all required components into a polished scholarly artifact, you demonstrate not only technical competence but also the ability to communicate value, justify investment, and prompt action. These skills prepare you to influence stakeholders, support strategic goals, and produce professional-quality deliverables that can stand on their own when decision-makers have limited time, reinforcing the real-world relevance of your academic work.
