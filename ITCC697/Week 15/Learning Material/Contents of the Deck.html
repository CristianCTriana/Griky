Rule of Three

Contents of the Deck

A deck should be no more than 20 slides. Make it a concise visual presentation that focuses on your proposed solution. The deck includes: 

  • Title slide: Generally includes logos or other branding and contact information. 
  • Problem statement: Just the statement, not a paragraph. 
  • Proposed solution: “In order to achieve X, we propose Y” tersely stated. 
  • Details of the solution: A few bullets describing the solution, not a manual. 
  • Details on how it solves the problem: ProblemàSolution for key aspects. 
  • Finances framing the cost/benefit/investment/ROI: Limited and key metrics. 
  • Proposed team structure: Key persons. 
  • Expected timelines: “If we begin now, we can deliver by [when?].” Use your rolled-up Gantt. 
  • Call to action for support/involvement: What are you looking for? Ask for it. 
  • Next steps: Propose logical next steps and seek ownership. 

All of this information comes from your prior work on the proposal and the business case. Here, you are taking the highlights and presenting them. 

Cutting and pasting long sections from the earlier documents and reading it to the audience in monotone is not usually engaging or interesting. Instead, seek out pithy phrasing to put on the screen and then speak to it. List key headlines and statistics from what your analysis has shown you. Show attention-grabbing statistics in charts and graphs. Show, don’t tell, on the slide. Provide the key points verbally. Direct them to the business case for the intimate details.
