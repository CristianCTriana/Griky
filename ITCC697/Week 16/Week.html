| Week 16 Relevance: Next Steps

Throughout ITCC697, the individual assignments, tools, and frameworks were designed to work together as a coherent way of thinking rather than as isolated tasks. The proposal, business case, and final pitch deck all stem from the same core problem and evolve through increasingly rigorous analysis and communication. Tools such as the integrative framework, A3, stakeholder analysis, change management models, risk registers, and ROI estimation provided structured ways for you to move from identifying a real organizational issue to justifying a practical, evidence-based recommendation. By repeatedly revisiting the problem through different lenses—current state analysis, alternatives, risks, value, and change readiness—you will learn how strong projects are built on alignment between theory, data, stakeholders, and execution, not just on good ideas.

These connections matter because they mirror how effective decisions are made in real organizations. The artifacts themselves are less important than the disciplined thinking behind them: framing problems clearly, grounding decisions in research, anticipating resistance, managing risk, and communicating value to decision makers. This course reinforces the scholar-practitioner mindset by showing how academic concepts translate into practical outcomes that can influence strategy and change. By the end of the course, you will not just have completed a capstone—you will be leaving with a reusable framework and transferable skills that can be applied to future projects, roles, and organizational challenges long after the course has ended.
