Week 2
The Proposal

A Useful Thought Guide to Start

The A3 Report came from the Toyota Production System (Chakravorty, 2009). It was called the A3 Report because of the size of the paper it was written on. A3 is an international paper size that roughly equates with the US 11x17 paper. 

The size of the paper is less important than sketching out your ideas. Today, lean and agile practitioners use a A3 process for problem solving. Here, it is presented as a tool to get your thoughts on paper before drafting the proposal. 

Use what you need to get your point across: charts, graphs, cartoons, and sketches. This is your document. It doesn’t have to be formal—unless your organization uses it. 

Sections to the A3 

Problem Statement

What are you talking about? This is the problem statement. It is a terse fact-based statement of the issue at hand. 

Background 

Why are you addressing the problem statement? What is the context? 

Current State 

What are the current conditions? Where do things stand today? What are the problems in evidence? 

This is a great way to show charts, graphs, or pictures of the issue. Visuals help a great deal in making a problem stand out. 

Analysis 

What are the root causes of the problems? The charts and graphs in the Current State should point to what is causing the underlying problem in your problem statement. 

Proposal 

What is your proposed solution? How will it achieve the target? The target should relate to your problem statement based on the Current State and Root Cause analysis. 

Deliverables and Goals 

This is a statement of what you propose to do and what the key benefits from doing it will be. 

Resources 

Who and what do you need to accomplish your task? This includes an estimate of roles that will be involved as well as a rough estimate of how long you will need them.  

This section also includes a rough cost estimate. 

Risks and Issues 

This section is a preliminary view of what could go wrong.  

The A3 is a tool. It is not the final deliverable, it is a sketchpad for your idea. It simply provides a framework for you to clarify your thinking.
