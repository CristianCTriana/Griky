Name of the week 

Deliverables and Goals, Resources, Risks and Issues, and Conclusion

Deliverables and Goals

  • What will be done
  • Phases of delivery
  • Expected outcomes
  • How long it might take

Resources

  • Who do you need on the team
  • How much of their time and for how long
  • Rough estimate of budget or investment to be consumed
  • What other resources might be necessary

Risks and Issues

Relates to the proposed project, not the broader organization.

  • Biggest risks to the project
  • Worst issues facing the project
  • Highest fear factor for failure

This is not an exhaustive list across all risk dimensions but rather the chief risks facing the project.

Conclusion

The conclusion of the proposal is the call to action. It reiterates the problem statement, touches upon the analysis, and recommends a solution. The solution is presented and its expected benefits are highlighted. A timeline and budget are proposed to deliver those benefits, and a request is made for support to proceed.

Sometimes, however, a proposal isn’t simply delivered and accepted. As a proposer, you must be prepared for some iterative development on the proposal. The sponsor you’ve approached may require some changes before agreeing to champion it. The same process may occur as the proposal is more broadly shared. Other stakeholders may have other feedback. This is a natural process within any organization.

Getting the proposal accepted is an exercise in communication and flexibility. It is part of the matching up of the decision situation with the project or system characteristics.

Professional Takeaway: Successfully navigating the proposal approval process enhances your knowledge of the organization you are attempting to influence, as well as increases your exposure to other members of that organization. Done well, it enhances your personal brand and leads to further success in the organization.
