What to Expect in Week 1

A project proposal is a persuasive document that provides a high-level view of a project’s purpose, scope, objectives, budget, and timeline.

Your organization may have different requirements for making a project proposal. For this course, you will produce a project proposal that covers the core ideas common to any project proposal, regardless of format.

This module walks you through these core ideas.

| Week 2 Relevance: The Proposal:

This week’s content connects the purpose of a project proposal with the thinking process behind it. The proposal is not just a formatted document; it is a persuasive tool designed to help decision makers understand a problem, see why it matters, and agree that your idea is worth pursuing. The Integrative Framework reinforces this by showing how a successful proposal must align the demand side (the decision situation, stakeholders, and context) with the supply side (the proposed solution, data, and knowledge). If these two sides are not clearly connected, even a technically sound idea can fail to gain approval. The proposal, therefore, becomes the bridge between recognizing a problem and securing permission to solve it.

The A3 framework ties these ideas together by giving you a structured way to think before you write. By walking through the problem statement, current state, analysis, and proposed solution, the A3 helps ensure that each section of the proposal logically builds on the one before it. This disciplined approach prevents jumping to solutions too early and keeps the proposal focused on evidence, impact, and feasibility. Ultimately, these tools matter because they teach you how to communicate complex IT problems clearly, justify your recommendations with data, and frame your ideas in a way that aligns with stakeholder priorities—skills that are essential not just for this course, but for professional practice in IT leadership and decision making.
