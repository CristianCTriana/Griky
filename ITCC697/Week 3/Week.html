What to Expect in Week 3

A business case is a formal document that describes a problem or opportunity and proposes a solution for it. It details the current state as well as the evidence as for why it should change. Additionally, it provides a detailed analysis of the current situation as well as the proposed solution, including supporting evidence and data. It then lays out the prospective return on investment. Tentative timelines and resource requirements are also proposed. Once approved, the business case is the foundation of the project to follow.

For this course, the business case is the main artifact used to assess your success.

This module covers the rationale behind a business case. The next module covers the business case in detail.

| Week 3 Relevance: The Business Case:

A business case brings together ideas about problems, evidence, decisions, and outcomes into a single, coherent argument for action. Rather than simply proposing an idea, it requires you to clearly define a problem or opportunity, explain why change is needed, and analyze possible solutions using data, research methods, and organizational context. The “why, what, how, when, and who” structure ensures that the problem, solution, and implementation are aligned, while the integrative framework emphasizes aligning the demand for a solution with the supply of decision support. In this way, research methods, theory, and practical constraints are not separate tasks, but interconnected tools used to understand the situation, evaluate options, and support informed decision making.

These ideas matter because organizations rarely act on ideas alone—they act on well supported cases that demonstrate value, feasibility, and impact. By learning how to construct a business case, you will develop skills that go beyond writing a document: you will learn how to think critically about evidence, anticipate decision-maker needs, and connect technical solutions to organizational goals. The focus on design, implementation, and success criteria highlights that a solution is only valuable if it is adopted and produces real improvements in cost, quality, or efficiency. Ultimately, this module helps you understand that success in IT and systems work depends not just on having good ideas but on doing the analytical, communicative, and organizational work required to turn those ideas into lasting results.
