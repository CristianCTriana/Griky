Details of the Business Case

A Business Case

A business case is the primary way organizations decide whether a project is worth pursuing. In this course, your business case is also the primary assessment and the culmination of your work. This lesson walks you through each required section of a formal business case so you understand not just what to include, but why each part matters to decision-makers.

  • A business case is a detailed document of up to 50 pages that provides the justification for pursuing a project including its feasibility, viability, and benefits.
  • It explains the problem, discusses alternatives, recommends a solution, and explains how it will be implemented.
  • It includes an executive summary, problem statement, detailed analysis of the current state, alternative solutions including cost/benefit analysis, recommended solution, and a proposal for implementation.
  • It provides all the details for answering the basic questions: What is it? What is it for? Why is it important? What do you need to get it done? What do you get out of doing it?

Outline of a Business Case

The sections of a business case follow a logical progression: defining the problem, analyzing the current state, proposing and justifying a solution, and demonstrating its value. The outline below shows the major components you will develop over time. Each section builds on the previous one to support a clear, evidence-based recommendation.

  • Executive Summary: What the proposal is for
  • Background: This is the current state and the why it should change
  • Detailed Analysis: In-depth discussion of the current state
  • Solution: This is the future state, what needs to be done
  • Alternatives: What else was considered
  • Deliverables and goals: These are the outcomes and benefits
  • Resources: People, money, and time you think you need
  • Risks: What could go wrong
  • Rewards: What is the expected ROI?
  • Conclusion: Selling the sizzle, the bang for your buck
