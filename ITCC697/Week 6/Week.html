What to Expect in Week 6

A careful review of the current state is important to the business case. You have made a problem statement in the proposal. In this section of the business case, you explore that problem or opportunity in detail.

The idea here is to understand the many dimensions of your problem and its impacts. Some business cases rely only on intra-organizational information. Here, the view is that broader perspectives are better. What do the academics say? What are your competitors doing? How about the big consultancies?

The information about the problem and how it is situated not only within your organization, but also within the broader context of your market sector, are important to understand and summarize for the business case.

| Week 6 Relevance: Detailed Analysis

The ideas in Week 6 connect by framing the Detailed Analysis of the Need as a structured, evidence based examination of an IT problem that mirrors a formal literature review. You move from restating the problem to systematically exploring its scope and impact using measurable indicators such as cost, quality, and cycle time. This analysis is strengthened by integrating multiple perspectives—scholarly research, industry practices, and internal organizational data—rather than relying on anecdotal evidence. By asking what literature, industry, and management say about the problem, you synthesize evidence across sources, helping you understand how different forms of information collectively define the current state of the issue and reveal its underlying drivers.

These ideas matter because strong business cases depend on credibility, context, and clarity. A detailed analysis allows you to demonstrate not only that a problem exists, but also why it is significant within your organization and the broader market environment. By understanding industry trends, societal changes, new technologies, and management perspectives, you can anticipate impacts beyond the immediate problem area, including both monetary and non monetary consequences. This approach shows decision makers that your proposed actions are informed by rigorous research and a deep understanding of organizational operations, increasing the likelihood that your recommendations will be taken seriously and approved.
