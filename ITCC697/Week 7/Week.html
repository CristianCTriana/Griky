| Week 7 Relevance: Value in Projects

Understanding value in projects requires seeing how operational improvements, financial measures, and human impacts work together rather than in isolation. The “big three” benefits—cost, quality, and cycle time—show how projects create tangible value by reducing waste, improving outputs, and delivering results faster. These improvements often lead to ideal outcomes such as increased profits, higher sales, and customer growth. At the same time, projects also generate soft benefits, including employee engagement and customer satisfaction, which support long-term organizational success even when they are harder to quantify. Together, these benefit categories help you see that project value is both measurable and experiential, affecting financial performance as well as organizational culture.

Financial tools such as return on investment (ROI), discount rates, and full-time equivalent (FTE) calculations connect these benefits to real-world decision-making. ROI helps you compare expected benefits to costs, while discount rates account for the changing value of money over time, reinforcing why timing matters in project outcomes. FTE calculations translate labor effort into consistent cost estimates, supporting realistic planning and resource allocation. These tools matter because they allow you to justify projects, set benchmarks for success, and align analysis and execution with organizational goals. By linking benefits to financial and staffing metrics, you can clearly understand how project value is evaluated, communicated, and defended within a business case.
