Week 8
Change Management

Popular Models

Organizations approach change in different ways depending on the size, scope, and people affected. To support decisionmaking in a business case, it is important to understand multiple change management models and when each is most effective. The following models are widely used frameworks that help leaders and project teams plan, communicate, and sustain change.

Widely used models such as Kotter’s 8-Step, Lewin’s 3-Step, and ADKAR provide effective strategies for guiding organizational change. After reading about them in your text this week, consider when they would each be best used.

Kotter’s 8 Step Model

Kotter’s model focuses on leadershipdriven, largescale change, where building urgency and maintaining momentum are critical.

  1. Establish a sense of urgency
  2. Form a powerful guiding coalition
  3. Create a vision
  4. Communicate the vision
  5. Empower others to act on the vision
  6. Plan for and create short-term wins
  7. Consolidate improvements
  8. Institutionalize new approaches

Lewin’s 3 Steps of Change

Lewin’s model emphasizes transition over time, making it useful for changes with a clear beginning and end.

  • Unfreezing: Motivation for change.
  • Moving: Making the change.
  • Freezing: Preventing reversion.

ADKAR

The ADKAR model centers on individual adoption, highlighting the human factors that determine whether a change succeeds or fails.

  • Awareness of the need for change
  • Desire to support and participate in the change
  • Knowledge of how to change
  • Ability to implement required skills and behavior
  • Reinforcement to sustain the change

From a business-case perspective, what stakeholder group benefits most from each model?

Change Process Flow

Regardless of the model used, organizational change follows a general progression from an existing state to a new way of operating. Understanding this process helps stakeholders recognize where resistance may occur and how change management activities support movement toward the desired future state.

1. Status quo ante

How things operate before the change.

2. Recognition

Something needs to be changed.

3. Change management

Orchestrating the change.

4. Acknowledgement

The change has made a difference.

5. Status quo post

How things operate after the change.
