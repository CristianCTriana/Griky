| Week 8 Relevance: Change Management

Change management brings together the human, organizational, and strategic dimensions of change by focusing on how people move from a current state to a desired future state. While tools like business cases and change control manage the logic and structure of change, change management addresses the attitudes, beliefs, and behaviors that determine whether a change is accepted and sustained. As you analyze change initiatives, concepts such as stakeholder recognition and desire emphasize that people must first understand why change is necessary and then be motivated to support it. Resistance—rooted in habit, complacency, entrenched leadership, or outdated systems—is not simply an obstacle to remove but a signal that human concerns must be acknowledged and addressed. Understanding these dynamics helps you anticipate challenges and design change efforts that are realistic, ethical, and effective rather than purely technical solutions imposed from the top.

The major change models—Kotter’s 8 Step Model, Lewin’s 3 Step Model, and ADKAR—connect by offering complementary perspectives on how change unfolds at different levels. Kotter emphasizes leadership, urgency, and momentum for large scale organizational transformation, Lewin frames change as a transition that must be stabilized to prevent regression, and ADKAR focuses on individual adoption through awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, and reinforcement. Together, these models show you that successful change requires alignment between organizational vision, structured processes, and individual readiness. This matters because applying the wrong model—or treating adaptive challenges as purely technical problems—can increase resistance and undermine outcomes. By understanding how these ideas fit together, you are better equipped to evaluate change situations critically and select approaches that fit the type of change, the stakeholders involved, and the long term goals of the organization.
