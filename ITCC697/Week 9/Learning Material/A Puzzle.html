A Puzzle

The “9 Dots” puzzle is a classic puzzle that many of you may have already seen. It is used here to illustrate the challenges involved in generating solutions to a given problem.

The problem: Without lifting your pen from the paper, connect all the dots in the diagram below with four straight lines.

Difficulty Factors

Kershaw and Ohlsson (2004) propose three classes of difficulty that interfere with forming a solution to a given problem.

Perceptual Factors

  • How much of the picture can you see?
  • How does the problem fit into a broader picture?

Process Factors

  • Why this problem?
  • Are there more like it?
  • How long might it take to solve?

Knowledge Factors

  • Is this like something you’ve encountered before?
  • How is it different?

These factors operate together and effectively obscure the solution.

Constraints

Many have a self-imposed constraint of staying within the square formed by the nine dots.

  • The perceptual factor is thus limited to that square.
  • The process factor assumes only tracing the line within that square.
  • The knowledge factor impacts the other two because there is, in fact, no such constraint.
