Challenging the Status Quo and Comparing Alternatives

Challenging the Status Quo

Challenging the status quo requires intentionally questioning existing practices and assumptions. The following prompts are designed to help you move beyond habitual thinking, build support for change, and use evidence to evaluate alternative approaches.

"What habits are your organization relying on?"

Question assumptions

"What opportunities lie beyond complacency?"

Reframe the need as opportunity

"Who can you win over to support the change?"

Build a coalition

"Can you show how complexity carries its own cost?"

Gather data and evidence

Comparing Alternatives

Marshall (2019) presents the RAID model (p. 12). These four dimensions can be used to classify and compare the various alternatives you consider:

Select each tab to learn more.

Risks Assumptions Issues Dependencies

Risks

Potential negative impacts.

Assumptions

Something taken to be true.

Issues

Something ongoing that must be managed.

Dependencies

Something relied on or required.

The chief considerations among the alternatives must include a financial dimension as well as a complexity dimension. So, to extend the model, add:

  • Cost
  • Complexity

A tabular format comparing the different alternatives and ranking them according to the dimensions above helps to simplify the presentation of the information.

Alternatives and Trade-offs

Every choice involves tradeoffs, including the decision to take no action at all. This section encourages you to think expansively about what else might be done, from technical solutions to organizational or human-centered approaches.

What else might be done?

  • “Do nothing” is one alternative — at what cost?
  • “Buy vs. Build” is another — at what cost?
  • Offer different solutions:
    • Technical solution with an existing product
    • Technical solution with a new product
    • Training
    • Staffing
