Comparing Alternatives

Marshall (2019) presents the RAID model (p. 12). These four dimensions can be used to classify and compare the various alternatives you consider: 

Risks 

Potential negative impacts 

Assumptions 

Something taken to be true 

Issues 

Something ongoing that must be managed 

Dependencies 

Something relied on or required 


The chief considerations among the alternatives must include a financial dimension as well as a complexity dimension. 

So, to extend the model, add: 

  • Cost 
  • Complexity 

A tabular format comparing the different alternatives and ranking them according to the dimensions above helps to simplify the presentation of the information. 

Alternatives and Trade-offs 

Every choice involves tradeoffs, including the decision to take no action at all. This section encourages you to think expansively about what else might be done, from technical solutions to organizational or humancentered approaches. By examining alternatives side by side, you can better assess their implications and justify the path you recommend. 

What else might be done? 

  • “Do nothing” is one alternative—at what cost? 
  • “Buy vs. Build” is another—at what cost? 
  • Offer different solutions 
    • Technical solution with an existing product 
    • Technical solution with a new product 
    • Training 
    • Staffing
