| Week 9 Relevance: Alternatives

This week’s ideas connect around a central insight: many problems feel difficult not because they lack solutions, but because we unknowingly limit how we see and approach them. The nine dot (9 Dot ) puzzle illustrates how perceptual, process, and knowledge factors work together to obscure alternatives. When we assume constraints that do not actually exist—such as staying inside the “box”—we narrow our thinking and overlook viable solutions. The module shows that these same dynamics appear in real organizational problems, where habits, complacency, entrenched management, and outdated technology function like invisible boxes that shape decisions. Recognizing these limiting frames is the first step toward generating meaningful alternatives rather than defaulting to familiar or comfortable options.

These ideas matter because effective decision making depends on the ability to challenge assumptions and evaluate alternatives systematically. By reframing problems, questioning the status quo, and intentionally looking beyond perceived constraints, leaders can surface a broader range of options. Tools like the RAID model—along with added considerations of cost and complexity—help compare alternatives using evidence based reasoning rather than intuition alone. This approach ensures that choices such as “do nothing,” “buy vs. build,” or investing in training or staffing are assessed for their trade offs and long term implications. Ultimately, the goal of generating alternatives is not simply to find a solution, but to identify the best course of action among several well reasoned choices.
