What to Expect in Week 3

In this module, you will learn to break down the essential elements of a Project Charter and Statement of Work (SOW) while understanding how these documents align projects with an organization’s broader strategic goals. The lesson highlights that projects operate within a larger system of portfolios, programs, and operations, and that their value is measured by how well they contribute to meaningful organizational outcomes. By studying these connections, you will develop the ability to look beyond task completion and focus on how projects create sustained value and long-term impact.

A real-world example of the importance of a well-crafted project charter and SOW can be seen in an organization implementing a new enterprise software system. In this scenario, a clearly defined charter formally authorizes the project, identifies the executive sponsor, and establishes high-level goals tied to business needs. At the same time, a detailed SOW outlines the specific deliverables, scope of boundaries, timelines, and vendor responsibilities. Because expectations are clearly documented from the start, misunderstandings are minimized, scope creep is controlled, and the project team has a shared understanding of success, significantly reducing the risk of delays, cost overruns, and stakeholder conflict.

This week’s focus highlights the distinction between “authorizing the work” and “defining the work.” The project charter serves as the formal authorization to begin, granting the project manager authority and aligning the initiative with organizational objectives. The Statement of Work, by contrast, defines what work will be performed, how it will be completed, and what will be delivered. Understanding the difference between these two documents helps ensure that projects are properly approved and clearly scoped, setting up a strong foundation for effective planning and execution.
