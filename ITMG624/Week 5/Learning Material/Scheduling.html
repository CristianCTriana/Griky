Week 5 Processes of Realistic Scheduling and Estimation Accuracy

Scheduling

This week, you are focusing on one of the most critical aspects of project planning: developing realistic schedules and producing accurate estimates. Even the strongest project ideas will fail if timelines and cost expectations are not achievable. When schedules are overly optimistic or estimates lack supporting logic, projects struggle regardless of team skill or effort.


Course Objectives

This lesson aligns with CO-5, which emphasizes how scheduling and estimation accuracy influence project outcomes. Two learning objectives guide your work this week:
  • LO-5.1 focuses on applying scheduling techniques such as critical path analysis and resource leveling to create timelines teams can realistically follow.
  • LO-5.2 emphasizes evaluating estimation methods, including bottom-up, analogous, and parametric estimating, and understanding how each affects project duration, cost, and planning accuracy.

Before applying specific tools, it is important to understand the governance framework described in PMBOK® Guide, Chapter 2.4. Governance provides the structure for how projects are approved, planned, and evaluated, ensuring accountability, transparency, and alignment with organizational goals. Strong governance supports realistic scheduling and estimation by requiring evidence-based planning and consistent decision-making standards that stakeholders can trust.

In practice, scheduling techniques help transform plans into achievable timelines.

For Example

In a construction project, the critical path may include site preparation, foundation work, framing, and inspections. Resource leveling ensures that the same construction crew is not scheduled for overlapping tasks across multiple phases.

By mapping task dependencies and aligning them with actual resource availability, the project manager develops a schedule that is both efficient and realistic, reducing delays caused by overcommitment or poor sequencing.

Estimation methods further refine project planning by determining how long tasks will take and what resources they require. In a software development project, a project manager might use bottom-up estimating for coding tasks, analogous estimating for testing based on prior projects, and parametric estimating for documentation using hours-per-page ratios. By comparing these approaches and selecting the most appropriate method for each task, the project manager balances accuracy with efficiency, supporting informed decisions and realistic expectations.

Common Pitfalls

Common pitfalls in scheduling and estimation include:

  • Relying too heavily on generic templates.
  • Underestimating resource constraints.
  • Failing to update estimates as conditions change.

These issues can be avoided by using historical data, involving stakeholders early, and regularly reviewing schedules and estimates as the project evolves. As you work through this week’s case study, focus on clearly explaining how scheduling and estimation techniques support effective planning, reduce risk, and contribute to overall project success.
