Governance Supporting Scheduling and Estimation

Consider a large IT infrastructure project to migrate an organization’s data center to the cloud. The project team initially proposed an aggressive six-month timeline, but the governance framework required approval of detailed schedules and resource estimates. Through this oversight, leadership identified gaps in staffing, vendor availability, and system dependencies, prompting adjustments that extended the timeline to nine months and included contingency buffers. By applying the governance framework, the project avoided unrealistic deadlines, resource conflicts, and costly rework, ultimately delivering a successful migration on time, within budget, and with minimal disruption to operations. This example demonstrates how governance structures provide the oversight needed to ensure scheduling and estimation are both realistic and strategically aligned.

Scheduling vs. Estimation: Week’s Focus

This week, also distinguishes between scheduling techniques and estimation methods. Scheduling techniques determine when project activities will occur and how tasks relate to one another, including sequencing, dependencies, and critical path analysis. Estimation methods, on the other hand, focus on how long or how costly activities will be, drawing on historical data, expert judgment, or algorithmic models. Understanding this distinction is critical, as accurate schedules rely on reliable estimates, and governance frameworks help ensure both are grounded in realistic assumptions that support project success.
