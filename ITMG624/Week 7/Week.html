What to Expect in Week 7

Greetings, students.

As you move toward the end of your course, this week you are focusing on what project success really looks like and how to measure it meaningfully. While success might sound simple, the PMBOK® Guide reminds us that it involves far more than finishing a project on time or staying under budget. Real success comes from strong planning, effective communication, stakeholder alignment, and using the right tools to track progress and make informed decisions. Your goal this week is to bring together everything you have learned so far and connect it to the bigger picture of managing and guiding projects toward lasting, valuable results.

This module sets the foundation for organized execution, ensuring that project tasks are coordinated and aligned with objectives. The Delivery domain focuses on producing outcomes that provide value to stakeholders, while the Measurement domain emphasizes tracking performance and using data to guide decisions. By studying these areas, you learn to integrate execution and assessment dynamically, ensuring projects are performed efficiently, deliver tangible results, and continually improve through informed evaluation.

Scheduling vs. Estimation: Week’s Focus

Consider a hospital implementing a new electronic health record (EHR) system. The project team identified key success factors, such as user adoption, data accuracy, and minimal disruption to clinical operations. They also established performance metrics, including system uptime, number of errors reported, and staff training completion rates. By monitoring these metrics against the defined success factors, the team identified areas needing support, adjusted training programs, and coordinated additional IT resources. The project launched successfully, achieving high user satisfaction and operational efficiency, demonstrating how integrating success factors and metrics drives meaningful outcomes.

This week’s focus also introduces the distinction between success factors and performance metrics. Success factors represent the conditions or behaviors that must be in place for a project to achieve its objectives, such as leadership support, clear goals, or stakeholder engagement. Performance metrics, on the other hand, are measurable indicators used to evaluate whether the project is meeting those success factors. Understanding the difference ensures that project managers not only know what leads to success but also how to monitor progress and make evidence-based adjustments.

As you reflect on your own experiences and case studies, consider how clearly defining success factors and establishing appropriate performance metrics could enhance the likelihood of your projects achieving their intended outcomes. Applying these concepts allows you to move beyond simple task completion toward producing real value for stakeholders and the organization.

As you move forward, identify one success factor or performance metric in your current or recent project that you could improve. Set a goal to implement at least one change based on this week’s lesson to ensure your project work consistently drives meaningful, measurable results.
