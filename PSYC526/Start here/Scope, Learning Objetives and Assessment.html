Scope, Learning Objectives, and Assessment

| Course Scope

This graduate course is a study of psychopathology from a current diagnostic, cultural, and theoretical perspective. Students will critically examine current classification systems (DSM-5-TR and ICD-11) with a special emphasis on how sociocultural, historical, and contextual issues inform our understandings of normality, expressions of psychological symptoms, and our diagnostic and assessment practices. Culturally informed assessment and formulation, including the DSM-5-TR Cultural Formulation Interview, will be discussed. We will cover anxiety disorders, mood disorders, trauma- and stressor-related disorders, personality disorders, cultural syndromes, and global mental health. The course will be research-based with a strong emphasis on evaluating and understanding different cross-cultural views of mental disorders and practicing diagnostic reasoning and decision-making on course-relevant case material. The student will be prepared to critically evaluate and understand the complexities of psychopathology, formulate case conceptualizations from a culturally responsive lens, and discuss ethical and structural issues that impact access to care and mental health disparities.
