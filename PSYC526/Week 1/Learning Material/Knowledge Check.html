Week 1 - Knowledge Check

Victoria is a 34-year-old woman who recently moved from Guatemala to the United States. She presents to a community health center after episodes of trembling, crying, and shouting during family disputes. Victoria reports that these episodes, which she characterizes as being triggered when “my nerves are overwhelmed,” are a culturally normative way of responding to interpersonal conflict in her family and community. She denies believing her reactions to be pathological, but reports feeling distressed by how they are being interpreted by others in the United States. When Victoria’s clinician, unfamiliar with her cultural idiom of distress, considers diagnostic possibilities involving panic symptoms, Victoria can clarify that her mother and aunt have had similar reactions, and “people at home know what it means.” Victoria denies persistent worry, avoidance behavior, or physiological anxiety in the absence of interpersonal precipitants.

Question 1: Victoria’s episodes best illustrate which Week 1 concept?

Correct answer: B. A culturally normative idiom of distress related to family dynamics.

Question 2: The clinician’s initial reaction reflects the importance of Week 1 content on:

Correct answer: B. Defining normality and abnormality within cultural context

Question 3: If the clinician applied the DSM-5-TR definition of mental disorder strictly, what additional feature would be necessary before diagnosing Victoria with a disorder?

Correct answer: C. Clinically significant distress or impairment

Question 4: Which diagnostic framework would be most likely to classify Victoria’s reactions based on global clinical utility and cultural applicability?

Correct answer: A. ICD-11

Question 5: nterpreting Victoria’s episodes solely as “panic attacks” represents a risk of:

Correct answer: B. Cultural mislabeling of normative distress

Question 6: Victoria’s explanation that her reactions are understood as “normal” in her family highlights which central Week 1 theme?

Correct answer: D. Normality and abnormality as culturally defined constructs
