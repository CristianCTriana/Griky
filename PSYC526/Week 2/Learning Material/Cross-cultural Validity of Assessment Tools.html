Cross-cultural Validity of Assessment Tools

The cross-cultural validity of measures can be systematically addressed by evaluating linguistic translation, conceptual equivalence, and metric comparability across settings. For cross-cultural validation, this involves several steps including forward/back translation, cognitive interviewing to assess understanding of the items, pilot psychometrics (factor structure, reliability), and differential item functioning analyses to see if items have different meaning/patterns across groups (Zhao et al., 2024).

Recently, several methodological frameworks and 10-step guides have been proposed for rigorous multilingual and multi-country instrument adaptation (Zhao et al., 2024).

The need for this is clear since many of the most common screening tools were developed in WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) countries, and may either miss culturally specific idioms of distress or overweight somatic vs. affective manifestations that may be found in other groups.

Systematic reviews suggest that some of these brief tools have acceptable cross-cultural psychometric properties when they have been adapted carefully (Zhao et al., 2024), while others have low construct equivalence and biased thresholds; there has been increasing interest in both transdiagnostic and dimensional measures (which may generalize better across populations), and developing culturally grounded validity tools developed in co-creation with the communities in question (such as Indigenous-led validation instruments) (Vize et al., 2023). The implication for clinicians and researchers is to assess instruments not only for translation, but for cultural content validity, and to report psychometrics for relevant ethnocultural groups.
