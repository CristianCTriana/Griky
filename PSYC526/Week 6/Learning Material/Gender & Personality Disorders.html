Personality Disorders & Cultural Norm

Gender & Personality Disorders

Gender also informs several dimensions of PD diagnosis, including presentation, measurement, clinical judgment, and stigma. Epidemiological studies show men to be diagnosed more often with ASPD or narcissistic patterns, whereas women are diagnosed more often with BPD or histrionic personality disorder. Emerging research, however, indicates that these patterns are partly driven by gendered stereotypes around emotion, aggression, dependency, and relationality (Bozzatello et al., 2024).

For Instance: Antagonistic or entitled traits may be viewed as assertive or normative for men but as pathological narcissism for women. Affective instability may be more likely to be labelled as borderline in women and externalizing in men.

Diagnostic Judgment & Ethics

Diagnostic judgment has been found to be impacted by gendered assumptions in studies where symptom descriptions are held constant, such as clinical vignettes and chart review studies. Research on psychopathy has similarly shown gender bias where women who present with callous or aggressive features are often judged more negatively for transgressing gender norms and where men may be overlooked for vulnerable narcissism or internalizing forms of PD (Bozzatelo et al., 2024). These dynamics have been described as entangled with broader cultural and structural forces around the meanings and contexts of power, vulnerability, and relationship formation, leading to ethical implications about how psychiatric classification enshrines gender roles.
