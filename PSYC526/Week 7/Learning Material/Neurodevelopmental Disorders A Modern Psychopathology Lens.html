Neurodevelopmental Psychopathology

Neurodevelopmental Disorders: A Modern Psychopathology Lens

Neurodevelopmental disorders are a group of disorders that manifest early in life, characterized with developmental variations in cognition, attention, learning, social communication, motor skills, and adaptive behaviors (APA, 2022). Neurodevelopmental disorders differ from neurocognitive disorders, where patients experience some sort of acquired decline from their original level of functioning. Neurodevelopmental disorders are unique in that they are diverse, dimensional, and chronic, with different symptoms predominating and functional expectations changing throughout one’s life.

Biopsychosocial models of neurodevelopment disorders involve genes, neurobiology, cognition, behavior, academic skills, culture, and even sociopolitical factors. Models such as developmental psychopathology, neurodiversity, and the Research Domain Criteria (RDoC) are moving towards viewing ADHD, autism, and learning disabilities as dimensional continuums of attention-regulation systems, social cognition, sensory processing, and academic learning rather than distinct categories. Moving forward, the newest version of the ICD has tried to move away from purely categorical diagnoses by basing the neurodevelopmental disorders module on impairment and rating severity, as well as noting that ADHD, autism, communication disorders, and specific learning disorders frequently co-occur (WHO, 2022).

From what we know about co-morbidity of neurodevelopmental disorders, making purely categorical diagnoses can be difficult.

For Example: Autism is often comorbid with ADHD and language disorder. ADHD is highly comorbid with learning disorders, mood and anxiety disorders, as well as disruptive behavior disorders. Specific learning disorders share risk factors with both ADHD (i.e., attention) and language disorders (i.e., language and executive function).

This supports the idea of a shared polygenic risk and/or neurocognitive underpinning across neurodevelopmental disorders. Neurodevelopmental disorders do not occur in a vacuum. There are documented differences in prevalence across countries in neurodevelopmental disorders and even within the United States based on culture, stigma, availability of healthcare, educational supports, and structural differences.
