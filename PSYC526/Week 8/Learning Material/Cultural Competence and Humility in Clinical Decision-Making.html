Cultural Competence and Humility in Clinical Decision-Making

Efforts to promote cultural competence have been lauded throughout training and clinical practice as efforts to address disparities and increase the therapeutic alliance. However, most recently, clinicians have moved toward implementing cultural humility (i.e., committing to an ongoing process of self-reflection and critique, awareness of power imbalances, and willingness to learn from clients) rather than simply meeting checklist-style competencies (Abe, 2020).

Cultural humility has been found to predict positive outcomes among clients such as better engagement in care and satisfaction, especially among clinicians providing care in multicultural contexts.

Another facet of clinical work that benefits from cultural adaptations is clinical decision-making. Culturally responsive clinical decision-making means consistently recognizing and integrating clients’ cultural identities and explanatory models into both assessment and treatment planning (Dixon et al., 2023; Kuo, 2024). This can be operationalized through the use of culturally adapted assessment instruments (e.g., DSM-5-TR Cultural Formulation Interview), flexible utilization of interpreters, and integration of traditional sources of support when it is appropriate and desired. Notably, being culturally responsive also requires us to identify our own biases as clinicians and understand how larger systems may advantage or disadvantage individuals from certain cultural groups. By doing this work, we can minimize providing clients with misdiagnoses and recommending interventions that do not fit their cultural values.
