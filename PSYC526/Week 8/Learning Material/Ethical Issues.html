Integrated Case Formulation, Cultural Humility, and Ethics

Ethical Issues

In the field of psychopathology, ethics extends beyond confidentiality and informed consent. Diagnosis and Labeling concerns emerge as clinicians consider how their diagnosis may impact their client, specifically, how diagnoses can be used as tools for access to resources or conversely how diagnoses can be stigmatizing and have effects on identity (Ahad et al., 2023). Research has found that when diagnosing clients from diverse backgrounds, it is important that clinicians not diagnose without taking into consideration cultural context as to avoid pathologizing culturally informed behavior (Kaiser & Weaver, 2019; Kohrt et al, 2014). This includes the development of DSM criteria based on western populations that may not include culturally bound expressions of symptoms which could lead to over- and under-diagnosing in people of color.

Connected to Ethics in diagnosis are concerns around clinician cultural stereotypes (Kaiser & Weaver, 2019), clinical imposition of norms (e.g. pathologizing cultural displays of emotion or not recognizing the difference between cultural norms and culturally sanctioned dysfunction) (Moleiro, 2018; Ntumi et al., 2025) and power dynamics between clients and clinicians. 

Clinicians walk a fine line of utilizing their power and expertise to help the client while not overriding or making decisions for the client. It is important for counselors to be aware of power differentials and to follow ethical guidelines when working with diverse clients (APA Multicultural Guidelines). Some of these include, but are not limited to respecting cultural identity, not assuming cultural backgrounds based on appearance, and consulting with community resources when appropriate.
