Evidence-Based and Culturally Adapted Treatments

Evidence-based practice (EBP) in psychopathology integrates three pillars: best research evidence, clinical expertise, and patient preferences. Although many evidence-based treatments have been supported for treating disorders (e.g., CBT for depression, DBT for borderline personality disorder), research indicates that these treatments may be more beneficial after cultural adaptations are applied (Mishu et al., 2023). When therapists culturally adapt their treatment, they are modifying the content, style of delivery, and context of psychological interventions to be more responsive to their clients' cultural background and explanations of their illness (Mishu et al., 2023). Research via meta-analysis found that psychotherapies with cultural adaptations were more effective than their standard treatment when working with diverse populations.

For Instance

Cognitive restructuring can be modified to fit more collectivist cultural values, include more family or community members when making changes to one's thinking, and training therapists to communicate with clients in culturally sensitive ways.

These are just some examples of how evidence-based treatments can be culturally adapted but do not weaken the mechanisms of change.
