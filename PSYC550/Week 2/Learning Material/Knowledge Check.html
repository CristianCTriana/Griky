Knowledge Check

A therapist notices increasing sexual attraction toward a client and begins feeling guilty and anxious but chooses not to seek consultation or supervision. 

  • Identify the ethical risks in this situation. 
  • Which recommended strategies from Corey et al. (2020) should be applied? 

Therapist feels growing sexual attraction and avoids consultation 

Ethical risks 

  • Unmanaged countertransference, which can distort judgment and harm the client. 
  • Increased risk of boundary violations, including emotional dependency, inappropriate self‑disclosure, or sexual misconduct. 
  • Isolation and impaired decision‑making, because the therapist refuses to seek supervision. 
  • Risk to client welfare, which is the core ethical principle. 
  • Legal and licensure consequences if boundaries are crossed. 

Recommended strategies (based on general principles described by Corey et al.) 

  • (Paraphrased, not quoted) 
  • Seek supervision or consultation immediately. 
  • Increase self-monitoring, including journaling feelings and triggers. 
  • Re‑establish firm, clear boundaries in sessions. 
  • Consider referral if attraction compromises objectivity. 
  • Practice ongoing ethical decision-making using recognized models.
