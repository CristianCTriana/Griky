Week 2 Relationships and Boundaries

Multiple Relationships

A multiple relationship is one in which a practitioner is in a professional role with a person in addition to another role with that same person or another individual who is close to that person. The terms dual relationship and multiple relationship are used interchangeably.

These types of relationships occur when a professional assumes two or more (hence multiple) simultaneous roles with a client. For example:

Business partner & Practitioner Counseling friends or relatives Socializing with clients Borrowing or lending money

Perspectives & Guidelines

There are varying perspectives on multiple relationships. While some suggest benefits to the secondary relationship (Moleski & Kiselica, 2005), others note the power imbalance inherent in these relationships (Zur, 2007).

After such examination, the practitioner should only form multiple relationships when it is clear that such relationships are in the best interest of the client.

The ethical guidelines for multiple relationships are applicable to all roles of the psychologist including counselor/therapist, consultant, advocate, researcher, etc.
