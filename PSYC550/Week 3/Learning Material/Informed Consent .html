Informed Consent

Informed consent is a right of clients, and an ethical and legal obligation for the practitioner. The practitioner has an ethical duty to provide the client with information about their rights and responsibilities in all settings, including therapy, assessment, consultation, and research.

The informed consent should be written in a language that is reasonably understood by the person asked to consent. This includes ensuring that the language is appropriate to the client’s:

Developmental level Culture Education

Additional considerations may be the use of an interpreter for translation, and the adaptation of the document for individuals with disabilities that impact reading comprehension (Fisher, 2023).

Cultural sensitivity may include education or familiarization regarding the client’s rights and voluntary participation. Informed consent should be documented on consent or assent form and discussed to ensure comprehension.

In some settings, this may not be feasible including anonymous surveys or when there is a concern that confidentiality may be at risk (Fisher, 2023).
