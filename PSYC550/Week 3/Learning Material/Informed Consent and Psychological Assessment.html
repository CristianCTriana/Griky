Week 2 Informed Consent

Informed Consent and Psychological Assessment

When conducting psychological assessments in clinical, forensic, or organizational settings, Fisher (2023) recommends the informed consent include specific components to ensure transparency.

Type & Format

Includes cognitive, personality, competency, or employment tests, along with duration and specific tasks.

Purpose

Clear use in employment selection, custody, treatment, or disability determination.

Payment

Detailed cost breakdown and payment schedule.

Third Party Involvement & PHI

Identifies individuals or organizations (health care companies, employers, courts) that will receive results. These are covered under HIPAA’s Notice of Privacy Practices, requiring written documentation of protected health information (PHI) disclosures.

Learn more about the Notice of Privacy Practices [website]

Confidenciality limitations: Includes mandated reporting, duty to warn, parent/guardian access to minor records, and protection of online assessment records.

Forensic Evaluations vs. Therapy

Forensic evaluations differ significantly from assessments for therapy. Practitioners must include a clear statement that the assessment is not being used for therapy and may not improve the client’s mental health.

  • Findings are provided to the client’s attorney to understand their psychological status.
  • The report may be used for legal proceedings and admissible as evidence.
  • Participation is not 100% voluntary; termination may have legal consequences.
  • Recommendations and feedback review sessions are not conducted in forensic settings.
Organizational Settings

In organizational settings, the informed consent should clearly explain that practitioners are not required to provide test results in assessments used for employment selection, court-ordered testing, and security clearance determination (Fisher, 2023).
