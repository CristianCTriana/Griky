Week 2 Informed Consent

Informed Consent and Schools

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) of 1974 is a federal law that protects the privacy of student education records in all schools that receive funding under the US Department of Education.

In most cases, a student’s educational record may not be released without written permission from the parent or eligible student (US Department of Education, 2020).

Fisher (2023) identified specific situations where informed consent is not required (exceptions):

Health or safety emergencies Judicial orders or local authorities School officials with legitimate interest Accrediting agencies Schools where a student is transferring Parties related to student’s financial aid

HIPAA vs. FERPA

HIPAA regulations do not apply to records that fall under FERPA. This is because FERPA does not make a distinction between educational records and health records; all are treated as part of the student's educational record.

For more detailed information, visit the official FERPA webpage.
