Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act

The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) is a federal law intended to establish standardization and increase efficiency in health care, while giving clients more control over personal health information.

HIPAA contains specific provisions related to privacy, informed consent, and record transfer. The HIPAA Privacy Rule requires health care providers to establish policies and procedures to protect client health information.

HIPAA has four components: privacy requirements, electronic transactions, security requirements, and national identified requirements.

Privacy

Regarding privacy, healthcare practitioners should take reasonable protections to protect client information.

Electronic Transactions

Electronic transactions should allow providers to communicate in a common language with one another.

Security Requirements

HIPAA provides minimum security requirements for the protection of confidential information.

National Identifiers

For example, health care providers must use national identification numbers in transactions.
