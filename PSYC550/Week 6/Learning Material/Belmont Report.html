Week 6 Ethical Issues in Human and Animal Research

Belmont Report

In 1973, Congress held a series of hearings focused on ethical practice in research with human subjects and health care. As a result of these hearings, Congress passed the National Research Act of 1974 which created the National Commission for the Protection of Subjects of Biomedical and Behavioral. The purpose of the Commission was to develop an ethical framework for health care research that focused on the boundaries of the research involving human participants, risk-benefit criteria to determine appropriateness of research involving human subjects, guidelines for selection of human participants in research, and informed consent in research settings (Title II, Part A, , § 202(a)(1)(B)). This framework is identified in the Belmont Report in 1976 and focuses on three ethical principles: respect for persons, beneficence, and justice. The Belmont Report can be found here:

Respect for participants emphasizes the valuation of autonomy. Autonomy refers to the person’s freedom to make life decisions. This principle asserts a responsibility to protect those who have limited autonomy. Essentially, this principle illustrates the role of informed consent, which is comprised of information, comprehension, and voluntariness. The individual should have enough information to decide about participation in research. The individual should demonstrate the ability to process and understand the information as it relates to their own life. The individual has the freedom to choose to participate or decline participation, without coercion or external influence (including threat, reward, or compensation) (Pritchard, 2018). 

Do no harm underlies the principle of beneficence. Research should strive to maximize the benefits and minimize the risks to participants and society. Furthermore, inhumane treatment is never justified. Vulnerable populations deserve special scrutiny, and the informed consent should clearly identify the risks/benefits to this population.

Justice addresses the selection of research subjects. Subjects should not be selected based on ease of recruitment. Risky research should not include only those who are socially disadvantaged (Pritchard, 2018).
