Week 6 Ethical Issues in Human and Animal Research

Institutional Review Board

Research with human subjects or animals requires approval by an Institutional Review Board (IRB). The IRB conducts a review of the proposed research to determine if it is ethical.

Fisher (2023) delineates the four components of ethical research practice with human subjects and animals requires: 

Psychologists must know if IRB approval is required and from whom it is required, and research must be compliant with federal, state, and local laws. 
IRB applications must be accurate. 
IRB approval is required before the research. 
Research must follow the IRB approved procedures. Any changes to the research must be submitted for IRB approval prior to implementation. 

Psychologists must obtain and document written or verbal consent. The informed consent should include the following, as noted in APA Guideline 8.02 (APA, 2017): 

Purpose of the research, duration and procedure 
Right to decline participation and withdraw during research 
Consequences of declining or withdrawing 
Potential risks, discomforts, or adverse effects 
Prospective research benefits 
Limits of confidentiality 
Incentives for participation 
Point of contact for questions about the research and research participants’ rights 

Informed consent should be a reciprocal dialogue that occurs throughout the research process. Participants should have the ability to ask questions and discuss concerns.
