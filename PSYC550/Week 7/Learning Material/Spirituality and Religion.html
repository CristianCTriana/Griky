Week 7 Ethics in Multiculturalism, Diversity, and Spirituality/Religion

Spirituality and Religion

Spirituality and religion are complex topics in the context of the therapeutic relationship.

Spirituality includes moral, ethical, and existential issues that are not considered in a religious context.

Religion focuses on the expression of devotion to a deity. 

Early in the practice of psychology, religion and spirituality were not deemed appropriate topics. Today, practitioners and educators recognize that religion and spirituality may be facets of the client’s presenting problem. They are also sources of strength and support for many clients and may play a critical role in resolving the client’s issues. Helping professionals should seek training to develop competence to work within the client’s spiritual or religious framework (Corey et al., 2020).

Helping professionals often see clients who are dealing with various end-of-life decisions, including

living wills,
consultation with medical providers,
assisted suicide,
power of attorney,
hospice care, etc.

Encouraging the client’s autonomy is a vital aspect of ethical care when dealing with end-of-life issues. 

Helping professionals working with end-of-life issues should be knowledgeable of their respective professional organization’s guidelines and state laws surrounding these issues.

Rational suicide refers to a person’s autonomous decision to end life because of ongoing suffering related to a terminal illness.

Aid-in-dying occurs when someone self-administers a death-causing agent that was provided by another person.

Hastened death occurs when one ends life earlier than would occur without intervention.

Advanced directives are documents that preserve an individual’s independence when he or she is no longer able to make decisions for himself or herself. These documents include specific details about how people want to handle end-of-life issues, such as refusing treatment or continuing a life-sustaining treatment. Examples of advance directives include the living will and durable power of attorney for health care (Corey et al., 2020).
