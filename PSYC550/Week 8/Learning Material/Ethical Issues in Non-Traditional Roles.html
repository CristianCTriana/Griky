Week 8 Ethics in community psychology and organizations

Ethical Issues in Non-Traditional Roles

Helping professionals may serve in a variety of roles aside from that of traditional therapy role, including that of

advocate
change agent
consultant
adviser
facilitator of indigenous support and healing systems

In the role of advocate, the helping professional is willing to speak on the behalf of oppressed or marginalized clients.

As a change agent, the helping professional provides the client with coping strategies to manage environmental issues. In some cases, the helping professional may need to educate organizations on ways to adapt to be in line with the community or organizational needs. 

To date, there are no distinct ethical codes for professionals practicing in the community and organization. Psychologists are expected to follow the APA Ethical Guidelines. The following professional associations are the most recognized in the field. 

Society for Community Research and Action [webpage] Division 27 of the American Psychological Association 

Society for Military Psychology [webpage] Division 19 of the American Psychological Association 

Society for Industrial Organizational Psychology [webpage] Division 14 of the American Psychological Association 

Society for Consulting Psychology [webpage] Division 13 of the American Psychological Association 

As a consultant, the helping professional may educate the clients on skills needed to interact with a diverse culture.

In the role of adviser, the helping professional takes a social work approach by focusing on the ways that clients can develop skills to manage environmental issues.

Helping professionals need to be knowledgeable of the healing resources and work to develop networks with the indigenous support systems to facilitate treatment.
