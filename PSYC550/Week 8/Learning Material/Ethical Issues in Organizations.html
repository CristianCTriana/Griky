Week 8 Ethics in community psychology and organizations

Ethical Issues in Organizations

Psychologists are likely to encounter situations when APA Ethics Code conflicts with laws, regulations, or organizational demands. In these circumstances, Fisher (2023) proposes the following courses of action:

inform appropriate authorities of the conflict,
explain the ethical standard, and
provide recommendations for resolution that are congruent APA ethical principles and standards.

When the psychologist is not able to resolve the conflict, the code permits compliance with the legal or regulatory standard, except in situations that would result in human rights violations (Trestman, 2014). 

When the ethical guidelines conflict with the organizational rules, policies, or procedures, the psychologist should make known the commitment to the ethics code and take reasonable steps to resolve the conflict. These steps may involve efforts to change the organizational guidelines to reflect the APA ethics code. 

Fisher (2023) described ethical conflicts that commonly arise in prisons and military settings. In these contexts, psychologists may be in a dual role that requires treating detainees and protecting from harm. The psychologist’s legal obligations to the organization may conflict with ethical guidelines. In these situations, the ethical guidelines suggest the psychologist act in a manner that promotes human rights. The code does not require adherence to the law in cases when it would violate human rights. 

Please read the APA Resolution on APA, Psychology, and Human Rights [webpage] (2021). The APA has taken a firm stance on the role of psychologists in interrogations. Concerns are also raised for psychologists who are asked to evaluate the cognitive ability of individuals in death penalty cases (Fisher, 2023). 

Consulting psychologists also have a responsibility to promote human rights at all levels of an organization and identify procedures to reduce bias and advocate for all levels of the organization (Fisher, 2023). 

Psychologists who receive commercial funding for research face additional ethical challenges when findings are not consistent with the sponsor’s interests, including attempts to falsify data or prevent publication. In these cases, psychologists make known their commitment to the ethics code and develop a resolution that is consistent with APA standards (Fisher, 2023). 

Informed consent is viewed differently in the context of forensic assessments requested by the examinee’s attorney and in mandated assessments. For a discussion of informed consent in forensic settings, please refer to the article by Bush (2020). In mandated assessments, there is no informed consent. Participation is not voluntary. In these situations, the psychologist should provide a notification of purpose that includes an

explanation of the nature and purpose of the assessment,
who requested the assessment, and
who will receive copies of the assessment.
