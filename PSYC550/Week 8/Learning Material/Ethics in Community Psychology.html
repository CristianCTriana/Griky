Ethics in Community Psychology

The community mental health orientation views the community as the focal point for solutions. Community agencies include any for-profit and non-profit organization that provides psychological and social services to the community. Community workers include any individuals serving the community, including

clergy
helping professionals
nurses
artists
residents

Direct interventions, such as outreach and preventive education, support personal development, whereas advocacy interventions enhance the community. Community counseling programs should focus on enhancing an individual’s well-being by leveraging community resources, which in turn benefits the overall community. When working with individuals, it is important to consider what resources their community has to offer and where it falls short (Corey et al. 2020). 

Direct client services emphasize outreach to individuals at risk for developing mental health problems. Community service workers reach out to schools and communities that are open to receiving help. This group may include referrals from the legal system, religious/spiritual centers, and drug treatment facilities. Corey et al. (2020) suggest that direct client providers emphasize the development of coping strategies to manage external stressors.

Direct community services aim to develop a variety of skills through preventive education programs. The programs may include programs focused on parenting, life planning, value clarification, and interpersonal skills.

Indirect client services include client advocacy and consultation. Community workers are not only merely providing a service, but they are also developing collaborative partnerships with the community by taking action when there are factors that negatively affect the clients.

Indirect community services advocate for systemic change by influencing policy makers. These services seek change in the social environment to improve the mental and physical well-being of community members. 

Helping the Community 

Bemak (2013) believes that helping professionals should take the initiative to advance communities by challenging systemic inequities and leveraging their desire to make the world a better place.

The Social Justice Perspective, also known as the fifth force, focuses on changing policy and systems to improve systems. Social Justice proposes that all people have the right to equitable treatment and a fair distribution of social resources. According to the Social Justice Perspective, counseling aims to empower marginalized and oppressed individuals. It is not practical for many helping professionals to devote the time and attention to bring about significant institutional change; however, all helping professionals can invest some efforts toward change. 

It is important for helping professionals to gain competency in social justice and advocacy (Corey et al., 2020). In an advocacy role, the helping professional seeks to bring about changes in public policy, law and public perception. Hailes et al. (2021) identify a framework for social justice in psychology that delineates guidelines in three categories: interactional justice, distributive justice, and procedural justice.

Interactional justice focuses on power dynamics and emphasizes empowerment.

Distributive justice focuses on the priorities of marginalized communities and investing in preventive efforts.

Procedural Justice focuses on social system impacts to individual and community well-being.
