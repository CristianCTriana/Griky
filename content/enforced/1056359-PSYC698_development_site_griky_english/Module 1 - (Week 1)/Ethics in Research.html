Ethics in Research

The Importance of Ethics in Research using Archival or Secondary Data (drafted with the help of Microsoft Copilot)

When conducting any research in psychology, it is important to act within ethical guidelines established by the discipline. Ethical research practices not only protect participants and maintain public trust but also ensure the integrity and credibility of your findings.

Why Ethics Matter

Even when working with existing datasets, researchers must respect the privacy and confidentiality of individuals whose data is being analyzed. This includes safeguarding sensitive information (if applicable) and ensuring that data is not used in ways that could harm or disadvantage participants.

Ethical compliance helps prevent data fudging, misrepresentation, or selective reporting. Adhering to ethical standards ensures that your research findings are valid and reliable, contributing meaningfully to the field of psychology.

The American Psychological Association (APA) outlines clear ethical principles for research, including the responsible use of data, transparency in reporting, and respect for intellectual property. For detailed guidance, check out the APA Ethical principles of psychologists and code of conduct [webpage].

IRB Approval or Waiver: A Non-Negotiable Step

Before you begin working with any data—archival or otherwise—you must obtain either approval or a waiver from your institution's Institutional Review Board (IRB). The IRB's role is to review research proposals to ensure that ethical standards are upheld and that risks to participants are minimized.

No Data Analysis Without IRB Clearance

You cannot start manipulating or analyzing data until you have received official IRB documentation stating that your project is either exempt or approved. This is a strict requirement and protects both you and the people who contributed their data to research.

Documentation

Always keep a copy of your IRB approval or waiver.

Practical Reminders

  • Develop strategies to ensure ethical compliance throughout your research design and data handling.
  • Produce your thesis report in accordance with APA ethical principles.
  • If you have concerns or uncertainties about ethical issues in your project, discuss them with your professor or IRB representative in the process.
