What to Expect in Week 2

Cleaning archival or secondary data is a critical step in the research process. Since these data sources were collected for purposes other than your current study, they often contain inconsistencies, missing values, or irrelevant information. Proper cleaning ensures the validity and reliability of your findings.

  1. Assess Data Quality and Structure
    Begin by thoroughly reviewing the dataset’s structure and documentation. Check for completeness, consistency, and relevance to your research questions. Identify variables that are essential for your analysis and note any that may be missing or poorly defined.
  2. Handle Missing Data
    Archival datasets frequently have missing values. Decide on an appropriate strategy—such as imputation, deletion, or flagging—based on the extent and nature of the missing data. Document your approach to ensure transparency and reproducibility.
  3. Standardize Formats and Codes
    Ensure that all variables are coded consistently. Standardize date formats, categorical labels, and measurement units. This step is crucial for merging datasets or comparing across studies.
  4. Remove Duplicates and Irrelevant Entries
    Scan for duplicate records and remove them to prevent bias in your analysis. Exclude entries that do not pertain to your research question, or that fall outside your defined inclusion criteria.
  5. Address Outliers and Inconsistencies
    Identify outliers and data points that deviate from expected patterns. Investigate whether these are errors or genuine observations. Decide whether to correct, exclude, or retain them, and justify your choices.
  6. Document All Cleaning Steps
    Maintain a detailed log of all cleaning procedures, including decisions about missing data, coding changes, and exclusions. This documentation supports the integrity of your research and allows others to replicate your work.
  7. Ethical Considerations
    Respect privacy and confidentiality, especially when working with sensitive archival data. Follow ethical guidelines for data handling and reporting, such as those outlined by the American Psychological Association.
