Drafting Your Literature Review

Constructing a Literature Review for Your Thesis (drafted with help from Microsoft Copilot)

A literature review is a critical component of your master's thesis, serving as the foundation for your research hypothesis and demonstrating your understanding of the scholarly context.

You began working on your literature review in PSYC697 with your development of an annotated outline. As a general reminder of the process, here are some steps to conducting a literature review.

Your literature review should be anchored around this problem and the specific, testable hypotheses you intend to address. This focus will help you select and organize the most pertinent literature.

Review quantitative and qualitative studies relevant to your topic. Aim for a thorough review that not only summarizes existing research but also identifies gaps or inconsistencies in the literature. This process will help justify the need for your study.

Structure your review logically—typically moving from broad themes to more specific studies directly related to your research question. Use your annotated outline (developed in PSYC697) as a guide but be flexible as you may need to reorganize sections as your understanding deepens.

Don't just summarize studies—critically evaluate their methodologies, findings, and relevance to your research. Discuss how the literature supports or challenges your hypothesis and highlights theoretical frameworks that inform your study.

Explain the implications of previous research and how your study will contribute to the field. Clearly identify where gaps exist and how your thesis aims to address them.

Ensure your literature review is well-structured, logically organized, and adheres to APA formatting and citation standards. Maintain ethical compliance by accurately representing sources and avoiding plagiarism.

Draft your literature review as a standalone chapter (Chapter Two of your thesis). Revise for clarity, coherence, and depth. Seek feedback from your advisor or peers to strengthen your arguments and organization.
