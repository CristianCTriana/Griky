Module 5: Writing Your Discussion Chapter (Weeks 12 through 14)

Guidance for Writing the Discussion Chapter

Introduction (drafted with help from Microsoft Copilot)

The Discussion chapter is the place where you give your interpretation of the results of your analyses: What do these findings mean in the context of your research question(s)? How do these findings relate to previous research and theory? What do these findings mean in the field of psychology? In this chapter, you will also acknowledge the limitations of your study and make recommendations for future research.

A well-structured Discussion chapter follows a logical flow. Use clear subheadings (e.g., "Limitations of the Study," "Implications for Future Research") to guide your reader.

Steps to writing the Discussion Chapter:

Begin by succinctly summarizing your key results. Clearly state whether your data supported or refuted your hypothesis. Compare your findings to prior research discussed in your literature review. Did your results align with or differ from existing studies? This sets the stage for deeper interpretation.

Delve into the meaning of your findings:

  • If your hypothesis was not supported, discuss possible reasons. Consider whether you asked the right question, if your data was sufficient, or if there were limitations in your study design. Address any potential sources of bias or methodological weaknesses.
  • If your hypothesis was supported, explore the implications. How do your results contribute to the field? Do they confirm or challenge existing literature? Be balanced - acknowledge both strengths and weaknesses and avoid overgeneralizing.

No study is perfect. Thoughtfully discuss the limitations of your research, such as:

  • Study design constraints
  • Data or sample limitations
  • Potential biases
  • Issues in data collection or analysis

Explain how these limitations might have influenced your results and what readers should keep in mind when interpreting your findings.

Discuss how your findings might apply to other populations or settings. Be cautious - avoid making claims that go beyond your data. Clearly explain the reasoning behind any suggestions for broader applicability.

Situate your work within the broader field:

  • How do your findings add to existing knowledge?
  • Do they support, extend, or contradict prior studies?
  • What new insights do they offer for theory or practice in psychology?

Demonstrate your understanding of the field and your study's place within it.

Based on your results, propose logical next steps:

  • What new questions have emerged?
  • What are the gaps or unresolved issues?
  • How might future studies build on your work?

Offer specific, feasible suggestions for future research that follow from your findings.
